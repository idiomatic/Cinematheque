# Cinematheque

Local-first video libraries. Players on Apple devices, macOS, Linux, and Windows browse shared libraries and play each video from any source that holds it. There is no central server: each library's titles, metadata, and people's data replicate among devices, so every device works offline and catches up when it reconnects.

This file specifies what's built. How operations are written, merged, and synced, and the keys and fields the library writes, are in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md); plans and open questions are in [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md); the measurements behind the design are in [experiments](experiments/README.md); outside specifications are indexed in [docs/specifications](docs/specifications/README.md).

## Lexicon

One word for each thing, in the spec, the code, the command-line app, and the apps alike; stored keys and fields keep their names as data. The apps show people only the words marked † (in everyday senses); the rest are for developers, and the apps say what they mean instead.

* **Source†:** anything serving videos over HTTP (a NAS, a web server, a folder a device shares); a device's downloads are one, though not listed. Not "repository."
* **Copy†:** a file of an item, in one quality, that a source holds (stored as `held:<source>`). Not "holding."
* **Device†:** a running Cinematheque with keys of its own (an app, the command-line app); the one it syncs with is the other device. Not "peer."
* **Person** (shown as people†): a human, with a key and devices; a member†, once admitted to a library. Not "user" (but for what's stored: the data directory's and the Keychain's `user` seed, and certificates' and names' `user` fields).
* **Progress:** where a person stopped in an item, and its watched mark. Not "playhead"; how far a long operation has come is its steps.
* **Unavailable†:** a folder that held files and lists none (stored as `absent:`). Not "absent," "missing," or "not found."
* **Pair†:** what a new device does to be another of a person's devices; to come in as a new member, it joins†. Not "link."
* **Data directory:** a device's own directory, of its keys and replica. Not "home," which is taken for `$HOME` (but for what's stored: the Keychain's `com.rbrian.cinematheque.home` items).
* **Share†:** what a device does with its library and folders (its server serves them).
* **Delete†** takes files off a disk; **remove†** takes something out of a list or the library (as an unavailable folder's videos); **forget†** discards only what's known (as an address added, or where a person stopped).
* **Keep†:** to spare a download deletion; **download next†:** a series' next episodes, as it's watched (one of its choices of episodes to download).
* **Quality†:** one encoding of an item (stored as a source's `rendition` field). Not "rendition." **Version†:** an edition (a director's cut); **origin:** a quality's Blu-ray, web, DVD, or HDTV token.
* **Sync** and **crawl**, shown as catching up† (with devices) and looking for new videos† (in sources), together Refresh Library†.

## Core interface

* **Threads:** its functions belong to one thread but for long ones (crawling, syncing, describing), each on a connection of its own; only one connection writes as the device at a time, and the main one catches up (`Replica.refresh`) before acting. Strings passed in are copied if kept.
* **Long operations** commit as they go; `cq_data_version` changes when they have, `cq_steps` tells how far one has come (its phase, and steps done of how many), and `cq_prioritize` names titles (e.g., those on screen) for a crawl to describe first.
* **Serving and syncing:** `cq_serve` serves the library (and folders, each at a path of its own) on a task of its own until `cq_stop_serving` or `cq_close`; `cq_served` tells of the last sync it served (to which device, how many operations), and `cq_received` of the last push it took (from which device, how many operations won). `cq_sync`'s results name each device as it advertised itself, with how many operations were pushed to it; `cq_sync_known` syncs with the devices the last such sync found (and those added), without looking for them again.
* **Pairing:** `cq_pair_offer`, `cq_pair_check`, and `cq_pair_stop` offer a new device to pair, as another of a person's devices or to join, and `cq_pair` pairs one, returning its person's key and its own; `cq_name` names another device, as one just paired.
* **People and devices:** `cq_status` tells whether this device is paired as another of a person's devices. `cq_set_name`, `cq_devices`, `cq_nearby` (devices found nearby, with names, and addresses added), and `cq_device_address` (adding an address, or forgetting one) let apps show people and devices by name. `cq_remove_progress` removes this person's progress in an item, and `cq_remove_device` another device.
* **Downloads:** `cq_downloads_configure` sets where a device keeps downloads and the URL it serves them at; `cq_download` and `cq_download_next` say what to download; `cq_keep` keeps a download however it's watched; `cq_download_remove` deletes; `cq_downloads` tells all of it (what's held, and which are the only copies, wanted, chosen, kept, and watched since downloaded, free space, and how far reconciling has come); `cq_download_sizes` tells what downloading a title, season, item, or series' choice would come to (its files' total size, and what of it isn't here yet); `cq_reconcile` (long) fetches and deletes to match, and while one runs, another asks it to run again and returns; `cq_downloads_hold` holds new downloads back while a stream plays.

## Devices

* **Players:** every client, each able to sync and crawl as well as play.
* **Refreshing:** every device syncs with the others first, then crawls, so the catalog others have crawled and described arrives first and the crawl adds and describes nothing unchanged.
* **Static servers:** sources running no Cinematheque code, such as a consumer-grade NAS or a web server (Caddy, nginx, Apache).
* **Desktop devices:** macOS, Linux, and Windows players. They are sources too, sharing their local and downloaded videos, and differ from static servers only in what they hold and how long they stay online.

## Identity and membership

* **Data directory:** `~/Library/Application Support/Cinematheque` on macOS, `$XDG_DATA_HOME/cinematheque` (`~/.local/share/cinematheque`) elsewhere, or another by `--data` or `CINEMATHEQUE_DATA`, which the C interface, and so the app, honors too. It holds `identity` (the device's random seed), `user` (the person's seed, if paired as another's device), `library` (the founder's person key), and `replica.db`.
* **Keys:** a device's keys derive from its seed, and its person's from the person's seed, which the person's devices share; a device not paired as another's derives its person's seed from its own. The person's seed is a secret, given to another device only by pairing, and never shown.
* **Pairing:** a new device, before it has a library, shows a 6-digit code and offers to pair, to be another of a member's devices or to join (as a new member), serving `/pair` and advertising `pair` (`pair` or `join`) by DNS-SD. A member's device, given the code, sends it the founder's key, and if pairing as theirs, the person's seed, sealed with a key both derive by CPace (ristretto255, SHA-512) from the code and the offer's kind, so an eavesdropper learns nothing, each attempt tests one guess, and the kind can't be changed on the way. The new device answers with its person's key and its own, sealed the same way: a joining one's person is added as a member, and the member's device names the new device as it advertised itself (a generic name, as tvOS gives apps, by its host: `Not-Your-AppleTV.local` as "Not Your AppleTV"), under its person, until it names itself. It stops after 3 wrong codes, until it offers again with a new one.
* **Certificates:** a device's first write is its certificate, its person's signature over the library and the device key; its private stream has one of its own, which travels on its stream. Before writing, once a connection, a device writes any of its own that's missing or invalid afresh, at the current time so it wins; the apps, on opening the library, rewrite one they hold that doesn't verify without waiting to write, but never write a missing one first (a device whose replica was lost must sync first). Only other devices would notice one that's invalid, by refusing the whole stream.
* **Library:** its ID derives from its founder's person key. Members add people to a set; any member may remove another, except the current founder.
* **Handover:** the founder may hand the library over to a member, staying or leaving. Handovers are a numbered chain, each valid only if written by the previous generation's founder, so devices agree in any order.
* **Removal:** any member may remove a device, without its person, or a member, with every device of theirs it knows. Each of a removed device's streams (its own and its private one) is cut off where the remover had seen it (`revoked`, a field per stream): writes through there stand, later ones are refused, and devices serve it no more; a removed member's device the remover didn't know isn't admitted.
* **Repair:** a device that took later writes before hearing of a removal drops them, notes their fields, and repairs them from a device that refused them, fetching just those fields' winners with one shared proof per batch; a write only it held, which lost to a refused one, is lost.
* **Leaving:** a removed device is told so when it next syncs: the device refusing it signs a refusal (`removed`) over the library, the device, and its request's time, which it believes only if a member certified the signer and it doesn't know the signer removed. It then forgets the library and its identity (`cq_leave`; on tvOS, the Keychain's seeds too), stops the refresh under way, and returns to setup, saying why, to pair again as a new device. It still holds its person's seed, so keeping it out for good would take a new person seed.

## Replication

* **Operations:** each writes one field of one key. Fields merge last-writer-wins by hybrid logical clock, ties broken by device key; set elements merge by causal length.
* **Batches:** a device signs each batch of up to 1,024 consecutive writes (a transaction's) once, with Ed25519, over its library, stream, range (first sequence number and count), and the root of a BLAKE3 Merkle tree of their encodings, so any operation, or set of them, is verifiable by proof, and a batch can't be presented with another range.
* **Storage:** SQLite holds each field's winning operation (as columns, from which its encoding is rebuilt), interned device keys, signed batches, a version vector of each device's highest sequence number covered, fields awaiting repair, and the device's own state (`local`, e.g., crawl snapshots). It runs in WAL mode, and connections wait up to 10 seconds for one another's writes, since the command-line app's `serve` and `refresh` share its replica.
* **Sync,** both ways, started by either side: a device POSTs to `/sync` a request signed by its device, with its certificate, the time, and its version vector. The other device answers members only, within ten minutes of its own clock, with frames: per device, its winning operations above the vector, grouped by batch, each batch with its signature and the leaves of operations not sent; then its vector, which the device adopts only under the sender's signature (`S`) over the request's signature and every frame's header, so a response is believed only from the device that sent it, for that request, with no batch left out; and, in headers, its own device key and certificate.
* **Push:** the device then pushes what the other device lacks: a second POST to `/sync` (its own content type) of a signed request and the frames above the other device's vector, signed as a response is, leaving out private streams not the other device's person's, which the other device applies as it would a response. So a device that can reach another that can't reach it (behind a NAT) syncs both ways. Frames are gzipped; a device too old to take pushes refuses one, and is only pulled from.
* **Own private stream:** a device takes its own private stream's batches as its person's even before the certificate arrives, so a device whose replica was lost (as tvOS may purge one) gets its progress back from its person's other devices.
* **Admission:** a receiver applies a batch only if its device's certificate names a member, else holds it until the end of the response, then rejects it without claiming it in its vector. It refuses operations past a removed device's cutoff, handovers not from the previous founder, and private data not from its person's private stream.
* **Private data:** a person's progress (`p/<person>/...`) is written on each device's private stream, with its own key and numbering. Devices send private streams only to the devices of the person they're of, and leave them out of the vectors they send others, so private data travels only directly between a person's devices.

## Library

* **Keys:** `t/<m|s>/<normalized title>/<year or ->` for a title; below it `/v/<edition or ->` for a movie's version (its `edition` field naming it as written) or `/e/s03e17` for an episode (then `/v/<edition>` for an episode's version; `/e/s00-<normalized title>` for a special without a number, its `title` field naming it), `/p/<n>` for a part, and `/r/<quality>`, whose `held:<source>` field records the file's path, size, and modification time, empty when no longer held. A title's key derives from its parsed identity, so devices crawling independently agree.
* **Naming:** the parser reads Plex, Jellyfin, and Kodi conventions and download names: ID tags, years (the last year ends a title), editions, parts (after the year), extras, `S01E02`, `1x02`, air dates, season folders, and Kodi's compact codes (`Show 317 Title`) right after the series' title from the enclosing folder. Dots between digits are a title's own (`11.22.63`); a name of numbers alone without a year (e.g., a camera's) goes to review. A numberless file beside a series' episodes whose name extends the series' (`Babylon 5 The Gathering`), or in a `Specials` folder, is a special (season 0).
* **Qualities:** a file's quality is its source's, if named; else its name's technical tokens, canonically (resolution, origin, codec, dynamic range, e.g., `2160p remux hevc hdr`); else unknown (`-`). Two files of one quality in one source are numbered (`~2`) as duplicates.
* **Review:** among titles some source holds, close matches (a year off by one, missing, or with transposed digits; the same year, the same digits, and a spelling within two edits; or only diacritics differ) and duplicates. An alias (`aliases`) records that one title key is another's; it replicates, and an empty one undoes it.
* **Progress:** per person and item (version, episode, or part), above qualities: position and duration in milliseconds, and watched, set on reaching the last 5% or by hand. A position in the first 10 seconds isn't kept: recording one empties the position and duration (not the watched mark), so stopping at the start sticks. Removing progress empties all three.
* **Describing:** after each crawl, one file per movie and per season, across every source, describes it, unless the file that last did is unchanged; else the best (a sidecar, then an MP4, then another file; a movie's standard version before its editions). It runs after crawling, so titles show before covers, with 16 workers, prioritized titles first, committing every 24 jobs or 750 ms. A network failure is retried next crawl; a file that isn't a readable MP4 records an empty cover, so it isn't reread until it changes. MP4 metadata fills a title's `description`, `genre`, `released`, and `network`, and an item's `title` (an episode's), `description`, and `duration`; other episodes are described on demand.
* **Covers:** from a sidecar image by Plex, Jellyfin, and Kodi convention (`poster.jpg`, `folder.jpg`, `<movie>-poster.jpg`, `season01-poster.jpg`, a season folder's `poster.jpg`), else an MP4's, read by range requests that walk atom headers to `moov` and read `udta` whole (about 8 requests). A cover is recorded as a byte range of the file (`art`, or `art:s01`... for seasons, on the title's key), so players fetch it with one range request; images don't replicate. Its placeholder does: `thumb` (or `thumb:s01`...), a ThumbHash in base64 of its JPEG's DC coefficients (an eighth-size image, decoded without a full decode), or `-` for another format, rendered by `cq_thumbhash`; a cover described without one gets one from a single range request.
* **Tiles:** for browsing, a movie or a season (a season without a cover takes its series'), with this person's progress: watched if every item is, else the share of its running time played (watched items whole, so one stopped in its last 5% counts in full, and items partway to their positions; an item of unknown length takes the average of those known), with the milliseconds elapsed and the runtime; how many items it holds; and whether any item has progress (begun), which puts a season with an episode partway on Home and Played.
* **Watchlist:** per person, private like progress (`p/<person>/watchlist`, a field per movie's title key, or per season, `<title key>#<season>`, each season listed on its own).
* **Names:** people's and devices' (`n/<key>`'s `name`, and a device's `user`, its person's key), set by any member, shown in place of keys.
* **Facets:** titles held by some source, counted by kind, decade, and quality.

## Sources

* **Settings:** a source is a URL (percent-encoded as typed), with an optional quality name and a prefix of names to skip (default `,`), changeable later (applied at the next crawl). Its ID is a hash of the URL. Removing it clears its settings and copies for every device, and the remover's crawl snapshot.
* **A device's downloads:** a source of its own, `managed` (a replicated field), which no device crawls: each file fetched is recorded as its copy in the very quality it came from, so devices see it and play from it. Files go under their paths in the source they came from, into `<path>.part` resumed by a range request, never written past their size, and only within the folder. While a stream plays on the device, no download begins (one under way finishes), so as not to slow it.
* **What a device keeps** is its own (`local`): items wanted (a title or season wanted is its items as of then, each in the quality the player's picker shows), and per series, a choice of episodes, stored as `current` (the latest in progress, else the first unwatched), a count N ("download next": the latest in progress and the N unwatched after it, so the next is there before it ends, else the first N unwatched), `unwatched`, a season's unwatched (`s02`), or `all`, kept however they're watched; specials are left out but for a season's and all.
* **Done with:** a download watched since it was fetched (its watched mark's clock past the fetch), unless kept or all of its series is chosen: deleted, wanted no longer, only once another source holds it alike (its quality and size, not unavailable), at once if the device says so, else only as its space is needed, oldest fetched first; the only copy, never. Reconciling also forgets copies whose files are gone (deleted by hand, or the folder changed), and never leaves less than 20 GB free.
* **Listings:** a crawl reads an `INDEX` if there is one (a path per line, as `find . -type f ! -name INDEX > INDEX` writes them, optionally followed by tab-separated size and modification time; a directory's ending in `/`, as Cinematheque's server lists them, so an empty one shows it's still there), else JSON listings (Caddy's or nginx's), else HTML, reading only links' targets.
* **Crawls:** breadth-first with concurrent workers (16 by default), reusing a previous crawl's listing of any directory with only files whose modification time hasn't changed, then bringing the source's copies in line with what was found. A crawl that fails anywhere changes nothing.
* **Unavailable:** a directory that held files and is still listed but holds none (an unplugged drive's mount point, or a NAS's) is one field on the source, `absent:<directory>` (relative to it, empty for the whole of it), noting when, its copies kept, rather than an operation per file; the topmost of an unavailable subtree only. A crawl clears it once the directory lists files again, or is gone from its parent's listing (deleted, its copies going as deleted files' do), or a year on, as if deleted; or a person removes its copies, as if deleted. Players show a title whose copies are all unavailable dimmed, and try unavailable copies last.
* **Serving:** a device listens on the port given, else on 8008 or, if that's taken, any free port, and advertises the port it got, binding with `SO_REUSEADDR` alone, never `SO_REUSEPORT`, so no two processes share a port. It answers `/sync`, and optionally serves media directories, each at a path of its own (the command-line app's one at `/media/`, the apps' by folder): files with single byte ranges, `If-Range`, and `mtime-size` ETags; listings as JSON (Caddy's form, on `Accept: application/json`) or HTML; and, for the command-line app's, an `INDEX` with sizes, times, and directories, built at start and again on a request once it's a minute old, so drives plugged in or unplugged show. The apps' folders have no `INDEX`, as they change; each is opened afresh for each request, and one that isn't there (a share unmounted) lists empty at its root, so a crawl finds it unavailable rather than failing.
* **Discovery:** devices advertise `_cinematheque._tcp` by DNS-SD (Bonjour on Apple platforms, Avahi's compatibility library on Linux), named for the device's key (`cinematheque-` and its first 8 hex digits), so a person's devices are told apart and a device skips only itself, with the library's ID, the sync path, the person's key, and the device's and person's names in the TXT record, so a device of the library whose person isn't a member yet can be shown, by name, to add; a new device offering to pair advertises `pair` (`pair` or `join`), the pairing path, and its name instead.

## Command-line app

* **Commands:** [usage.txt](cli/src/usage.txt) lists them, and is their help. Devices pair and join only by code; an offer prints its code and the port it listens on, for a device that can't find it nearby (`pair CODE URL`). State commands are `NOUN VERB`, with verbs `list`, `show`, `add`, `set`, and `remove` where they apply, over nouns `person`, `library`, `member`, `device`, `source`, `title`, `alias`, `progress`, and `agent`; whole-app actions are single words (`crawl`, `sync`, `serve`, `refresh`). Options are named (`--port`, `--leave`); `ls` and `show` are shortcuts for `title list` and `title show`.
* **Usage:** placeholders are in capitals, named for what they mean rather than their type (`URL`, `--port PORT`). `-h` or `--help` shows usage, of one noun or action if given, as does a noun alone; a command line that isn't one shows the same and exits with status 2.
* **Downloads:** `title download` fetches a title's, a season's, or an item's files into a directory (the current one, or `--to`), as a device's downloads go (their paths, `.part` files, and only within the directory, since a crawling member's device chose the paths): in the quality given, else the one most of its items have; from its copies in copy racing's order, the next tried when one fails; a file already there at its size is kept.
* **Web client:** `serve --web` serves one at the root (embedded in the app: plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, no framework), off unless asked for, since anyone who can reach the port can use it. A browser acts as this device's person, having no keys: Home (Continue Watching, the watchlist), Movies, Series (seasons under their series' names), Watchlist, and Played, searchable, the apps' tiles with their marks (a tab shown chosen at once, its shelf built a screenful first and the rest a slice a frame, each tile's cover and placeholder fetched only as it nears the screen), and each shelf's counts in a status line, as the Mac's says them (a series' shelf counting its seasons and episodes too), fading out 3 seconds after they last changed and back when the pointer nears it, as the Mac's does, a problem staying until something replaces it; a title's page, as the apps' are laid out: Play or Resume on a row of its own, filled, the watchlist and watched marks quieter at its far end; a movie's progress, and each episode's in its row, with what's left and Start from Beginning (↺) beside it, which forgets the position, not the watched mark, as the Mac's does (no Start Over beside Play, as on the Mac); then an Episodes heading with a Season pop-up (the season named there, not in the facts) and, at its trailing edge as the Mac's, if there's a choice, a Quality pop-up (of what browsers play: MP4, M4V, or MOV, HEVC only where supported, best first, by the apps' short names), or a Versions heading with it, or for a movie of one version, it alone; and a season's episodes, described as it's shown, each once a page load; and playing as the apps do, full height with nothing over the video but the browser's own controls (the browser's Back or Escape leaving it, as a history entry; what's playing as the window's title) (fading, resuming 4 seconds before where it was left, saving every 10 seconds past the first 10 and where it's paused or left, marking it watched at its end, going on to the next, the next copy tried when one doesn't load). Under `/api/`, it answers the apps' tile and title JSON (shared with the C interface, in `views.zig`), covers (a source's byte range, fetched by the server, since sources answer other sites' scripts nothing, and cached for good), placeholders (ThumbHashes drawn as small BMPs), copy racing (`race`, `lost`), describing an episode (`describe`), and this person's writes (`progress`, `watched`, `watchlist`, `forget`); it fetches and races only URLs within the library's sources. A second after a browser's last change of this person's (not an episode described), it syncs with every device found or added, as the apps do after theirs.
* **Added addresses:** addresses added are kept in `local`, on that device only; `sync` without a URL, and `refresh`, pull from every device discovered or added.
* **Background:** on macOS, `agent add` loads two launch agents (`com.rbrian.cinematheque.serve`, keeping `serve` running, and `.refresh`, syncing with devices discovered or added, then crawling, hourly), logging to `~/Library/Logs/Cinematheque`.

## Apple players

* **Structure:** one `App` and root view (its data directory unopened, setting up, or browsing; at launch and once set up, naming, sharing, and refreshing), over a shared model, cover cache, playback session, title presentation, shelves, setup, and debug launch options; each platform's `Browser` and the rest apart (`#if os(...)`), and what else differs (the data directory, default names) in `Platform`. One Xcode target (Xcode 27's `project.xcproj` format), whose build phase builds the core for the platform and each architecture and links it into one object exporting only the C interface, so the compiler runtime Zig bundles can't clash with Xcode's.
* **Title presentation:** facts, runtimes, episode names, Play labels, the quality to play (its pickers giving each a short name, its resolution with HDR if it says so, "4K HDR", or Other for one of unknown quality; where two would read alike, each with what else its full name says, "720p Very Fast"; the full name, as its sources give it, as help), and the watchlist and watched marks, quieter than what plays (borderless on the Mac, icons alone on the Apple TV), the watched one showing its state (Watched or Unwatched), which pressing toggles. Start Over (`backward.end.fill`, labeled, before Resume, as a player's timeline starts at its leading edge) plays what Play would from the beginning.
* **Shelves:** Home, Movies, Series, Watchlist, and Played (everything with progress, in progress or watched), with their tiles, search, counts (a series' shelf counts its episodes too), and empty text. Remove from Played, in a tile's context menu, removes its progress: a season's on the Mac (as Remove from In Progress, Remove from Watched, or elsewhere Mark as Unwatched), a whole series' on the Apple TV, which shows one tile per series. The Mac's sidebar shows all but Home, with Played split in two: In Progress (Home's) and Watched.
* **Setup** (`SetupView`): pairing by a code this device shows (`OfferedCode`), to be another of the person's devices or to join someone's library, or creating a library; a code shows a digit to a box, in two groups of three, as it's typed on the other device. On the Mac, one form: Pair with Your Other Device and Ask to Join, each showing its code in place, and Create Library. On the Apple TV, tvOS-style steps: the app's name over three large choices (I Use Cinematheque on Another Device and Join Someone's Library, each a screen showing its code; Start a New Library). Neither app shows or takes keys.
* **Playing** (`FadingIn`), on every platform: fading to black while the stream warms up, paused (loaded, sought to its position, less 4 seconds when resuming, and prerolled), then playing and fading to the picture as its sound rises from silence; when left, fading to black as its sound falls, its position saved where that fade ends, so resuming replays the 4 seconds before it. A curtain (`Curtain`) covers both, each fade 0.5 seconds (`Platform.fade`). It starts at the position unless watched, saves it every 10 seconds past the first 10 and where it's paused or left (on the Mac, the first save reconciles downloads, so a kept series' next episode comes while this one plays), marks an item watched at its end, and goes on to the next.
* **Copies:** a download on this Mac plays from its file, first and unraced. Else the stream is first from the copy whose server answers first, among the chosen quality's available copies only, never another quality (copy racing, `cq_race`, in the core; with one copy, none): each copy's asked at once (`HEAD`, 1.5 seconds, or once one has answered, 250 ms more for a better-placed one, as a NAS waking), one that refused or didn't answer by the deadline in the last 10 minutes (as this device notes) asked last, so a device that's asleep costs a second or so, not a network timeout. The sources' servers' names are looked up once a launch's refresh is done (`cq_race_warm`), since a name's first lookup may outlast a race. When a copy doesn't load (a drive unplugged), the item's other copies follow in turn, the quality's, then its other qualities'.
* **Sharing:** the apps share the library (and the Mac its folders) while they run, serving on the port they got last, so other devices sync with them directly. They sync quietly (not shown as an activity) a minute after they last synced, and whenever they come to the front (as an Apple TV app returns from the home screen, never relaunched). After a change of their person's (pausing or leaving playback, a watched mark, the watchlist, Remove from Played), once changes stop for a second (and once any sync or other long operation under way is done, as it may have begun before the change), they sync with the devices the last sync found (`cq_sync_known`), pushing it to every device at once; when a device pushes its changes to them, they reload at once. For diagnosis, each shows the last exchange each way that changed something, in one line: what it received, from which device, and what it sent, to which device, and when.
* **Project:** Xcode's recommended settings but for script sandboxing (the core's build phase), the App Sandbox (the data directory it shares with the command-line app), the hardened runtime (until signing for distribution), and its macOS 15 minimum; a developer's signing settings stay in an ignored `Local.xcconfig`. The bundle's `Info.plist` allows plain HTTP and Bonjour on the local network.

## macOS player

* **Shelves:** Movies, Series, Watchlist, In Progress, Watched (what's watched through, and any with a download watched since), and Downloaded (what this Mac keeps); no All. The one shown last is shown again at launch (at first, In Progress), searchable.
* **Grid:** covers in fixed cells (148 points wide at any width, so a change in width reflows whole tiles and resizes none), movies 2:3 and each series' seasons square under its name (each season's cover whole, fit and centered, here and in the inspector). Each has a star at its top leading corner if on the watchlist, a watched mark (top trailing) or progress bar, and a download mark (bottom trailing) if this Mac holds any of it, or, if watched since, an hourglass (waiting for its space to be needed) or an orange mark (the only copy, for a person to delete or keep). A cover shows its placeholder until loaded (without one, a color with its title), then replaces it at once, without a fade; covers are fetched by range request, scaled with ImageIO off the main thread, and cached in memory and in the app's caches.
* **Keys and scrolling:** the arrow keys move the selection over the covers (left and right in order, up and down a row, into the next section at its nearest column), scrolling just enough to show it, and Return plays it, as double-clicking does. Each shelf has its own scroll view, and returns to its topmost title (the first at least half on screen) when shown again; the grid keeps that title, not an offset, at the top as its width changes and its rows reflow (the inspector opening or closing), putting it back only when the columns change, not at every width an animation passes through.
* **Inspector:** for the selected tile, a larger cover, Play or Resume (a movie, or a season's episode in progress, the latest if several, else its next unwatched, with the rest of the season queued), filling its row, with the watchlist and watched marks beside it (labeled where they fit, else icons alone), a quality picker in the heading of what it plays (Episodes; Versions; or for a movie of one version, under its details), metadata, and a movie's versions or a season's episodes (Season 0 is Specials), each playable (an episode with the rest of the season after it queued, so the first's plays the season from its start), described on demand. A movie's progress, and each episode's in its row, has Start from Beginning (`arrow.counterclockwise.circle.fill`) beside it, which forgets the position, not the watched mark, so Resume becomes Play.
* **Downloading:** each in one row in which no control appears or disappears, so nothing moves: a spinner keeps its place while hidden, and what doesn't apply is dimmed. A movie's row: Download, with its size ("Download 4.2 GB", all its files', in the quality picked), dimmed once it's here or coming; a spinner while it comes; and a trash button at the row's trailing edge, canceling while it comes, deleting once it's here, its help saying, once watched, whether it's waiting or the only copy. A season's row: a Download pop-up of the series' choice, one level, changed only by picking another (Nothing, Current, Next, Next 3, 5, or 10; then Unwatched in Season {n} (Unwatched Specials; another season's too, if that's the one chosen), Unwatched, and All, short since its label reads them, as "Download Next 3"), the open menu giving each choice's size (all its files') under a header of the space downloads may take ("212 GB free", above the floor kept free), the closed one only the choice (its own label); a spinner while any of the season's episodes asked for hasn't come; and a trash button at the trailing edge, deleting the season's downloads. A size is orange if what isn't here yet wouldn't fit (a menu item given any other color is drawn as if disabled). Deleting or canceling a download its series' choice asks for sets the choice to Nothing, so it isn't fetched again. An episode's row has its mark and a context menu, as a movie tile's has: Download, Keep Download, Stop Keeping Download, Delete Download (saying so if it's the only copy), and Cancel Download.
* **Crawling:** while a crawl runs, the grid reloads as it commits (polling `cq_data_version` each second), and the tiles on screen are described first.
* **Playing:** in the library's window, filling it over the browser (which keeps its place), with the toolbar hidden and the title bar transparent, its row (as QuickTime Player's) holding the window buttons, Back (or Escape, or the queue's end) returning to the library, its black fading away to it, and what's playing (a movie's name, or a series' with the episode's code and name), in a window title's size, centered on the window buttons' line, over a faint gradient, the row showing only as the player's controls do, while the pointer moves and for 3 seconds after. With ⌘ held (⌘-click, ⌘-double-click), in a window of its own, titled with what's playing, as many as wanted; player windows aren't restored at launch. Space plays or pauses, F toggles full screen, M mutes, C toggles captions (in the languages preferred, the rest of the queue too; a beep if there are none), and the left and right arrows skip 10 seconds, in an `AVQueuePlayer` in AppKit's `AVPlayerView`; while it plays, the display neither dims nor sleeps (an activity of its own, as AVPlayer's didn't hold).
* **Settings,** by what people do, with addresses under Advanced, in four tabs; names are ones everyone in the library sees. On launch the app names this Mac and you (the computer's name, the account's first name) if they have no names. Long operations run off the main thread.
* **People:** members by name, with the founder, you renamable in place, and devices nearby of the library whose people aren't members yet, to Add.
* **Devices:** your named devices, this Mac among them, each renamable in place and, but for this Mac, removable; sharing the library on the network while the app runs; New Devices, always shown, listing new devices nearby offering to pair or join, each with six boxes for the code it shows (in two groups of three, as the device shows it; pasting works, and only digits go in), pairing it at the sixth digit (and adding a joining one's person; a wrong code shakes the boxes clear, as the login window does) and moving it, animated, into Your Devices (or People); under Advanced, devices by address (beyond a NAT, say).
* **Sources:** Add Folder… and Add Server… (buttons, not a menu), and Refresh Library (a button, `arrow.clockwise`); each source's row ending in Show in Finder (a folder on this Mac) or Open in Browser (a server's listing) and Remove (a minus), as in its context menu. Folders on this Mac are served at `http://<Mac>.local:<port>/<folder>/` while the app runs and kept in the app's settings; one that isn't there (a drive unplugged, a share unmounted) is shown as unavailable and served empty, so a crawl marks it unavailable. Sources have readable names ("art › Movies › 2160p"); a quality and names to skip are under Advanced, with a source's unavailable folders, each with when it went and Remove Videos (a minus); devices' downloads aren't listed. If the app must serve on another port, it moves its folders' sources there.
* **Downloads:** the folder (`~/Movies/Cinematheque` unless chosen, as Apple's TV app keeps `~/Movies/TV`), served at `/downloads/`; what it holds and the space free; progress while downloading; and whether watched downloads go at once.
* **Toolbar:** the sidebar's toggle and the shelf's title at the leading edge; the inspector's toggle (⌥⌘I) and search at the trailing. Refresh Library (⌘R, in the View menu) syncs, then crawls, its steps showing as "Catching up with 1 of 2 devices…" and "Looking in 3 of 6 sources…". At launch, if there's a library, the app shares it, syncs, names itself if need be, then crawls, syncing before writing anything, so a device whose replica was lost learns its own writes back first.
* **Status bar:** along the bottom of the grid, floating over it, so fading moves nothing: the shelf's counts ("4,000 movies"; "12 of 4,000 movies match …" while searching), or what went wrong, at the leading edge; at the trailing, what a long operation is doing, with a progress bar, or else the last exchange each way that changed something ("Received 3 changes from firefly 1m ago · Sent 12 changes to Den just now", counting operations), unless a problem shows. It fades out 3 seconds after it last changed, unless something is running or wrong, and comes back when the pointer nears it.
* **Modelessness:** no spontaneous alerts, after the original Macintosh guidelines: like stealing focus, an alert nobody asked for interrupts. An action a person takes is confirmed first only if it's hard to undo: removing a source (saying how many videos go, on every device, its files untouched), since only adding it again and looking for its videos anew undoes it; and deleting a download that's the only copy, which nothing undoes. What goes wrong shows in the status bar (and in a bar along the bottom of Settings) in words, never the core's error names, each source by its name ("Looking for new videos failed for art › Movies › 2160p (and 5 more): the server has nothing at that address."), until dismissed, until the next action of its kind (refreshing, changing the library, reading it) succeeds, or until the device gains or leaves a library.

## Apple TV player

* **Data directory:** in the caches (the core's default on tvOS), the only place tvOS lets an app keep files, which it may purge; the seeds (`identity`, `user`, `library`) are kept in the Keychain too, and put back at launch, so a purge loses only what syncing and crawling rebuild.
* **Refreshing:** at launch, naming itself (the Apple TV's name) if it has no name, sharing the library (so its progress and watchlist reach the person's other devices), then syncing with devices found by DNS-SD, then crawling the sources itself.
* **Exchanges:** the last exchange with devices each way that changed something, shown for a few seconds in the corner after each.
* **Shelves:** a tab bar of Home (Continue Watching, then the watchlist, each row a focus section, so moving down from the tab bar enters the first row), Movies, Series (a cover per series, its progress over every season's running time), Watchlist, Played, and Search; covers six to a row, as cards, with progress and watched marks, and a star if on the watchlist (a series', if any season is).
* **Pages:** a movie's, over a blurred backdrop of its cover: Play or Resume (Start Over before Resume) and the watchlist and watched marks, on one row; versions, and its description; a Quality picker (if there's a choice; the best it plays unless one's chosen) in the Versions heading, or under the description for a movie of one version. A series': its seasons (the first not watched through outlined, specials last; each cover whole, fit and centered in its square), Play for the season's next episode, and its episodes under an Episodes heading with a Quality picker as a movie's, described as the season's shown. The quality is the best it plays: MP4, MOV, or M4V (not MKV or ISO), up to 2160p on a 4K display, else up to 1080p.
* **Playing:** full screen in `AVPlayerViewController`, started once per request (fading the page re-hosts it, so not in a view's task), Play pressed again over the black ignored until the player's on screen; AVFoundation and AVKit are loaded as the shelves first show. The page (and the tab bar) fades to black while the stream warms up, the session made once the fade's done; then the display switches to the video's frame rate and range (asked of the window by the app, if Match Content is on), blanking the screen; then UIKit presents the player over the black (AVKit's views don't belong in SwiftUI's), unanimated and ready, so it shows no spinner; then its picture fades in as its sound rises, once it's playing, not merely asked to (surround passed through, encoded, to a receiver can't ramp, and comes at once). Its remote skips and scrubs as the system's player does, and its info panel (and Now Playing) shows each item's title, description, and cover.
* **Leaving:** Menu (Back) fades it to black as its sound falls; then, its controls hidden, the display is given its own mode back, and while that switch runs (some 3 seconds, the screen blank), it's stopped and dismissed over the black page, which fades back in once the switch is done. Going to the background (the TV button), it pauses, silent, and comes back paused (AVKit's own resume held off), its sound rising when it's played.

## Debugging

* The apps log to the system's log (Xcode's console), under `com.rbrian.cinematheque`: playback's steps and their times (`playback`), and each crawl's per source (listing, listings read and reused, and recording) and describing's after (`crawl`), from `cq_crawl`'s results.
* Debug builds take launch options for driving the app unattended (in a VM by `open --env`; in the tvOS simulator by `simctl launch`, each prefixed `SIMCTL_CHILD_`): `CINEMATHEQUE_CODE_FILE` (a file each pairing code offered is written to, for a test to send from another device), `CINEMATHEQUE_SELECT` (a tile, e.g., `t/s/firefly/-#1`: selected on macOS, its title's page opened on tvOS), `CINEMATHEQUE_TAB` (tvOS: a tab, `movies`, `series`, ...; on both, in setup, a step, `pair` or `joinOther`, offering its code), `CINEMATHEQUE_DOWNLOAD_FLOOR` (the free space downloads keep, in bytes, for a VM's small disk; any build, read by the core), `CINEMATHEQUE_PLAY` (once a launch: a URL to play on macOS, with `CINEMATHEQUE_ITEM`, its item key, for its progress; any value on tvOS, to play the opened title).

## Implementation

* Zig 0.17+ for the core and the command-line app, with no dependencies beyond the system's SQLite and DNS-SD. Whatever waits (networking, sleeping, SQLite waiting out another connection's write, DNS-SD waiting for results) does so through Zig's `Io`, so it can be canceled; only what `Io` can't reach blocks outside it (SQLite's own disk I/O, and the system's name lookup, which copy racing avoids on Apple platforms by looking names up through DNS-SD).
* SwiftUI for Apple players.
* SQLite for each device's replica.
* Tests: the core's unit tests (`zig build test`; `zig build` compiles the C interface too), [cli/test.sh](cli/test.sh) (two devices and more, end to end), screenshots of the macOS player in a macOS VM (terrarium's `macos-27.2-quiet`), and of the Apple TV player in the tvOS simulator (an Apple TV 4K, 3rd generation).

## Scale

100,000+ video files: 4,000+ movies, and 500+ TV series of up to 30 seasons of up to 50 episodes.

## Risks

* App Review may resist publishing a library that encroaches on Apple's own, all the more so one that serves media.
* Outside metadata comes from providers outside Cinematheque, whose sources' terms (e.g., TMDB's charge for commercial use, IMDb's datasets for personal use only) bind them, not Cinematheque.
* GPL tools (`HandBrakeCLI`, GPL builds of FFmpeg) must run as separate processes, never linked, or they bind the license of the players and the shared core.
