# Cinematheque

Local-first video libraries. Players on Apple devices, macOS, Linux, and Windows browse shared libraries and play each video from any source that holds it. There is no central server: each library's titles, metadata, and people's data replicate among devices, so every device works offline and catches up when it reconnects.

This file specifies what's built. How operations are written, merged, and synced, the keys and fields the library writes, and the measurements behind the design are in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md); plans and open questions are in [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md); outside specifications are indexed in [docs/specifications](docs/specifications/README.md).

## Lexicon

One word for each thing, in the spec, the code, the command-line app, and the apps alike; stored keys and fields keep their names as data. The apps show people only the words marked † (in everyday senses); the rest are for developers, and the apps say what they mean instead.

* **Source†:** anything serving videos over HTTP (a NAS, a web server, a folder a device shares); a device's downloads are one, though not listed. Not "repository."
* **Copy†:** a file of an item, in one quality, that a source holds (stored as `held:<source>`). Not "holding."
* **Device†:** a running Cinematheque with keys of its own (an app, the command-line app); the one it syncs with is the other device. Not "peer."
* **Person** (shown as people†): a human, with a key and devices; a member†, once admitted to a library. Not "user" (but for what's stored: the data directory's and the Keychain's `user` seed, and certificates' and names' `user` fields).
* **Progress:** where a person stopped in an item, and its watched mark. Not "playhead"; how far a long operation has come is its steps.
* **Unavailable†:** a folder that held files and lists none (stored as `absent:`). Not "absent," "missing," or "not found."
* **Join†:** what a new device does to come into a library, by a code it shows; a member's device adds† it, as another of its person's devices (pairing† it) or someone new's (a new member). Not "link."
* **Data directory:** a device's own directory, of its keys and replica. Not "home," which is taken for `$HOME` (but for what's stored: the Keychain's `com.rbrian.cinematheque.home` items).
* **Share†:** what a device does with its library and folders (its server serves them).
* **Delete†** takes files off a disk; **remove†** takes something out of a list or the library (as an unavailable folder's videos); **forget†** discards only what's known (as an address added, or where a person stopped).
* **Download next†:** a series' next episodes, as it's watched (one of its choices of episodes to download).
* **Quality†:** one encoding of an item (stored as a source's `rendition` field). Not "rendition." **Version†:** an edition (a director's cut); **origin:** a quality's Blu-ray, web, DVD, or HDTV token.
* **Sync** and **crawl:** syncing goes on by itself, shown only as catching up† where it explains something (a removed device leaving); crawling is shown as looking for new videos†, which Refresh Library† does (syncing first).

## Components

* [core](core/README.md): the Zig library, and a static library with a C interface ([cinematheque.h](core/include/cinematheque.h), a Swift module by its [module map](core/include/module.modulemap)) whose results are JSON (see [Core interface](#core-interface)).
* [cli](cli/README.md): the command-line app, `cinematheque`, for macOS and Linux.
* [apple](apple/README.md): the Apple players, in SwiftUI: one multiplatform app, `com.rbrian.cinematheque`, for macOS 15 or later and tvOS 18 or later (see [Apple players](#apple-players)).
* [tools](tools/README.md): scripts alongside the apps, such as fetching a YouTube channel into a folder that reads as a series.

## Core interface

* **Threads:** its functions belong to one thread but for long ones (crawling, syncing, describing), each on a connection of its own; only one connection writes as the device at a time, and the main one catches up (`Replica.refresh`) before acting. Strings passed in are copied if kept.
* **Long operations** commit as they go; `cq_data_version` changes when they have, `cq_steps` tells how far one has come (its phase, and steps done of how many), and `cq_prioritize` names titles (e.g., those on screen) for a crawl to describe first. `cq_crawl` crawls every source; `cq_crawl_due`, for a refresh not asked for, those no device crawled within the library's interval, which `cq_crawl_every` sets and `cq_status` gives.
* **Serving and syncing:** `cq_serve` serves the library (and folders, each at a path of its own) on a task of its own until `cq_stop_serving` or `cq_close`; `cq_served` tells of the last sync it served (to which device, how many operations), and `cq_received` of the last push it took (from which device, how many operations won). `cq_sync`'s results name each device as it advertised itself, with how many operations were pushed to it; `cq_sync_known` syncs with the devices the last such sync found (and those added), without looking for them again.
* **Pairing:** `cq_pair_offer`, `cq_pair_check`, and `cq_pair_stop` offer a new device to be added, and `cq_pair` adds one, as another of this person's devices or a new member's, returning its person's key and its own; `cq_name` names another device, as one just paired.
* **People and devices:** `cq_status` tells whether this device is paired as another of a person's devices, and how many other devices its person has. `cq_set_name`, `cq_devices`, `cq_nearby` (devices found nearby, with names, and addresses added), and `cq_device_address` (adding an address, or forgetting one) let apps show people and devices by name. `cq_remove_progress` removes this person's progress in an item, `cq_remove_device` another device, and `cq_remove_self` this one, as it leaves.
* **Downloads:** `cq_downloads_configure` sets where a device keeps downloads and the URL it serves them at; `cq_download` and `cq_download_next` say what to download; `cq_download_remove` deletes; `cq_downloads` tells all of it (what's held, and which are the only copies, wanted, and chosen, free space, and how far reconciling, and the file under way, have come); `cq_download_sizes` tells what downloading a title, season, item, or series' choice would come to (its files' total size, and what of it isn't here yet); `cq_reconcile` (long) fetches and deletes to match, and while one runs, another asks it to run again and returns; `cq_downloads_hold` holds new downloads back while a stream plays.

## Devices

* **Players:** every client, each able to sync and crawl as well as play.
* **Refreshing:** every device syncs with the others first, then crawls, so the catalog others have crawled and described arrives first and the crawl adds and describes nothing unchanged. A crawl records when it was, on its source (`crawled`); a refresh not asked for (at launch, or on a timer, which waits a random time first so devices on timers don't refresh together) skips a source any device crawled within the library's interval (`settings`' `crawl`, for every device: every hour, 3 hours, day (the default), or week, give or take a 24th of it, drawn afresh each time, so devices that crawled together don't come due together), whose copies syncing brought, or every source if it's Manually; so a consumer NAS's disks spin up for crawling no more than chosen, and Refresh Library, and `crawl`, crawl them all, when a person knows there's something new.
* **Static servers:** sources running no Cinematheque code, such as a consumer-grade NAS or a web server (Caddy, nginx, Apache).
* **Desktop devices:** macOS, Linux, and Windows players. They are sources too, sharing their local and downloaded videos, and differ from static servers only in what they hold and how long they stay online.

## Identity and membership

* **Data directory:** `~/Library/Application Support/Cinematheque` on macOS, `$XDG_DATA_HOME/cinematheque` (`~/.local/share/cinematheque`) elsewhere, or another by `--data` or `CINEMATHEQUE_DATA`, which the C interface, and so the app, honors too. It holds `identity` (the device's random seed), `user` (the person's seed, if paired as another's device), `library` (the founder's person key), and `replica.db`.
* **Keys:** a device's keys derive from its seed, and its person's from the person's seed, which the person's devices share; a device not paired as another's derives its person's seed from its own. The person's seed is a secret, given to another device only by pairing, and never shown.
* **Pairing:** a new device, before it has a library, shows a 6-digit code and offers to be added, serving `/pair` and advertising `pair` (`new`) by DNS-SD. A member's device, given the code, decides what it is (its person, entrusted with the choice, as handing its person's seed to someone else's device would let it act as them): it sends the founder's key, and if adding it as its person's, the person's seed (else zeros), sealed with a key both derive by CPace (ristretto255, SHA-512) from the code, so an eavesdropper learns nothing and each attempt tests one guess. The new device answers with its person's key and its own, sealed the same way: a new member's person is added as a member, and the member's device names the new device as it advertised itself (a generic name, as tvOS gives apps, by its host: `Not-Your-AppleTV.local` as "Not Your AppleTV"), under its person, until it names itself. It stops after 3 wrong codes, until it offers again with a new one.
* **Certificates:** a device's first write is its certificate, its person's signature over the library and the device key; its private stream has one of its own, which travels on its stream. Before writing, once a connection, a device writes any of its own that's missing or invalid afresh, at the current time so it wins; the apps, on opening the library, rewrite one they hold that doesn't verify without waiting to write, but never write a missing one first (a device whose replica was lost must sync first). Only other devices would notice one that's invalid, by refusing the whole stream.
* **Library:** its ID derives from its founder's person key. Members add people to a set; any member may remove another, except the current founder.
* **Handover:** the founder may hand the library over to a member, staying or leaving. Handovers are a numbered chain, each valid only if written by the previous generation's founder, so devices agree in any order.
* **Removal:** any member may remove a device, without its person, or a member, with every device of theirs it knows. Each of a removed device's streams (its own and its private one) is cut off where the remover had seen it (`revoked`, a field per stream): writes through there stand, later ones are refused, and devices serve it no more; a removed member's device the remover didn't know isn't admitted.
* **Repair:** a device that took later writes before hearing of a removal drops them, notes their fields, and repairs them from a device that refused them, fetching just those fields' winners with one shared proof per batch; a write only it held, which lost to a refused one, is lost.
* **Leaving:** a removed device is told so when it next syncs: the device refusing it signs a refusal (`removed`) over the library, the device, and its request's time, which it believes only if a member certified the signer and it doesn't know the signer removed. It then forgets the library and its identity (`cq_leave`; on tvOS, the Keychain's seeds too), stops the refresh under way, and returns to setup, saying why, to pair again as a new device. It still holds its person's seed, so keeping it out for good would take a new person seed. A device may also leave of its own accord: it removes itself, cutting its streams off just past that removal (if its person is the founder and it's their only device, handing the library over first to a member, who's asked for), syncs so another device takes it (refusing to go on if none can be reached, and retried without writing it again), then forgets as above; with no one else in the library, it only forgets.

## Replication

* **Operations:** each writes one field of one key. Fields merge last-writer-wins by hybrid logical clock, ties broken by device key; set elements merge by causal length.
* **Batches:** a device signs each batch of up to 1,024 consecutive writes (a transaction's) once, with Ed25519, over its library, stream, range (first sequence number and count), and the root of a BLAKE3 Merkle tree of their encodings, so any operation, or set of them, is verifiable by proof, and a batch can't be presented with another range.
* **Storage:** SQLite holds each field's winning operation (as columns, from which its encoding is rebuilt), interned device keys, signed batches, a version vector of each device's highest sequence number covered, fields awaiting repair, and the device's own state (`local`, e.g., crawl snapshots). It runs in WAL mode, and connections wait up to 10 seconds for one another's writes, since the command-line app's `serve` and `refresh` share its replica.
* **Sync,** both ways, started by either side: a device POSTs to `/sync` a request signed by its device, with its certificate, the time, and its version vector. The other device answers members only, within ten minutes of its own clock, with frames: per device, its winning operations above the vector, grouped by batch, each batch with its signature and the leaves of operations not sent; then its vector, which the device adopts only under the sender's signature (`S`) over the request's signature and every frame's header, so a response is believed only from the device that sent it, for that request, with no batch left out; and, in headers, its own device key and certificate.
* **Push:** the device then pushes what the other device lacks: a second POST to `/sync` (its own content type) of a signed request and the frames above the other device's vector, signed as a response is, leaving out private streams not the other device's person's, which the other device applies as it would a response. So a device that can reach another that can't reach it (behind a NAT) syncs both ways. Frames are gzipped; a device too old to take pushes refuses one, and is only pulled from.
* **Own private stream:** a device takes its own private stream's batches as its person's even before the certificate arrives, so a device whose replica was lost (as tvOS may purge one) gets its progress back from its person's other devices.
* **Admission:** a receiver applies a batch only if its device's certificate names a member, else holds it until the end of the response, then rejects it without claiming it in its vector. It refuses operations past a removed device's cutoff, handovers not from the previous founder, and private data not from its person's private stream.
* **Private data:** a person's progress (`p/<person>/...`) is written on each device's private stream, with its own key and numbering. Devices send private streams only to the devices of the person they're of, and leave them out of the vectors they send others, so private data travels only directly between a person's devices.

## Library

* **Keys:** `t/<m|s>/<normalized title>/<year or ->` for a title; below it `/v/<edition or ->` for a movie's version (its `edition` field naming it as written) or `/e/s03e17` for an episode (`/e/s2024e0312` for one dated, a season a year, as Plex has it; then `/v/<edition>` for an episode's version; `/e/s00-<normalized title>` for a special without a number, its `title` field naming it), `/p/<n>` for a part, and `/r/<quality>`, whose `held:<source>` field records the file's path, size, and modification time, empty when no longer held. A title's key derives from its parsed identity, so devices crawling independently agree.
* **Naming:** the parser reads Plex, Jellyfin, and Kodi conventions and download names: ID tags, years (the last year ends a title), editions, parts (after the year, and after an edition's name: `- Extended Part 1`), extras, `S01E02`, `1x02`, air dates, season folders, yt-dlp's `[youtube-ID]` tags, and Kodi's compact codes (`Show 317 Title`) right after the series' title from the enclosing folder. Dots between digits are a title's own (`11.22.63`); a name of numbers alone without a year (e.g., a camera's) goes to review. A numberless file beside a series' episodes whose name extends the series' (`Babylon 5 The Gathering`), or in a `Specials` folder, is a special (season 0).
* **Qualities:** a file's quality is the first folder within its source, as named, if the source names qualities by folder (`Movies/HQ 1080p30 Surround/Alien (1979).m4v`; that folder isn't read as the file's name); else its source's, if named; else its name's technical tokens, canonically (resolution, origin, codec, dynamic range, e.g., `2160p remux hevc hdr`); else unknown (`-`). Two files of one quality in one source are numbered (`~2`) as duplicates, shown as "1080p 2" (an unknown one's "Other 2").
* **Review:** among titles some source holds, close matches (a year off by one, missing, or with transposed digits; the same year, the same digits, and a spelling within two edits; or only diacritics differ) and duplicates. An alias (`aliases`) records that one title key is another's; it replicates, and an empty one undoes it.
* **Progress:** per person and item (version, episode, or part), above qualities: position and duration in milliseconds, and watched, set on reaching the last 5% or by hand. A position in the first 10 seconds isn't kept: recording one empties the position and duration (not the watched mark), so stopping at the start sticks. Marking an item unwatched empties its position and duration too, so it plays from the beginning; removing progress empties all three.
* **Describing:** after each crawl, one file per movie and per season, across every source, describes it, unless the file that last did is unchanged; else the best (a sidecar, then an MP4, then another file; a movie's standard version before its editions). It runs after crawling, so titles show before covers, with 16 workers, prioritized titles first, committing every 24 jobs or 750 ms. A network failure is retried next crawl; a file that isn't a readable MP4 records an empty cover, so it isn't reread until it changes. MP4 metadata fills a title's `description`, `genre`, `released`, and `network`, and an item's `title` (an episode's), `description`, and `duration`; other episodes are described on demand, and other versions too (their durations alone, so an edition's file doesn't speak for its movie).
* **Covers:** from a sidecar image by Plex, Jellyfin, and Kodi convention (`poster.jpg`, `folder.jpg`, `<movie>-poster.jpg`, `season01-poster.jpg`, a season folder's `poster.jpg`), else an MP4's, read by range requests that walk atom headers to `moov` and read `udta` whole (about 8 requests). A cover is recorded as a byte range of the file (`art`, or `art:s01`... for seasons, on the title's key), so players fetch it with one range request; images don't replicate. Its placeholder does: `thumb` (or `thumb:s01`...), a ThumbHash in base64 of its JPEG's DC coefficients (an eighth-size image, decoded without a full decode), or `-` for another format, rendered by `cq_thumbhash`; a cover described without one gets one from a single range request.
* **Tiles:** for browsing, a movie or a season (a season without a cover takes its series'), with this person's progress: watched if every item is, else the share of its running time played (watched items whole, so one stopped in its last 5% counts in full, and items partway to their positions; an item of unknown length takes the average of those known), with the milliseconds elapsed and the runtime; how many items it holds; and whether any item has progress (begun), which puts a season with an episode partway on Home.
* **Watchlist:** per person, private like progress (`p/<person>/watchlist`, a field per movie's title key, or per season, `<title key>#<season>`, each season listed on its own).
* **Names:** people's and devices' (`n/<key>`'s `name`, and a device's `user`, its person's key), set by any member, shown in place of keys.
* **Views:** what players show comes named and decided, so no player reads a key or decides alone (`views.zig`, for the C interface and the web client alike): a title's items in order (each season's episodes by number, specials last), its seasons in that order, which item Play plays of each season or of a movie's versions (`next`: the latest in progress, else the first unwatched after the last watched, else the first unwatched, else the first), each quality's resolution (`height`, for players to rank what they play by); and items with an episode's season and number, a dated one's air date, a label ("Episode 3", "Special", "March 12", or a version's name, "Standard" or its edition; a part's with its number, "Part 1" or "Director's Cut, Part 1"), and a name in a list ("3. Title"); and each quality's short name for a picker (its resolution, with HDR if it says so, "4K HDR", or Other for one of unknown quality; where two of a title's would read alike, each with what else its full name says, but for options (`--colorspace=bt709`) unless only they tell them apart, "720p Very Fast").
* **Facets:** titles held by some source, counted by kind, decade, and quality.

## Sources

* **Settings:** a source is a URL (percent-encoded as typed), with an optional quality name, or qualities by folder (`quality-by`), and a prefix of names to skip (default `,`), changeable later (applied at the next crawl). Its ID is a hash of the URL. Removing it clears its settings and copies for every device, and the remover's crawl snapshot.
* **A device's downloads:** a source of its own, `managed` (a replicated field), which no device crawls: each file fetched is recorded as its copy in the very quality it came from, so devices see it and play from it. Files go under their paths in the source they came from, into `<path>.part` resumed by a range request, never written past their size, and only within the folder. While a stream plays on the device, no download begins (one under way finishes), so as not to slow it.
* **What a device keeps** is its own (`local`): items wanted by hand (a title or season wanted is its items as of then, each in the quality the player's picker shows, or if it lacks it, as players play it, the nearest lower, else higher; a version of parts, its parts; an item's key, that file alone), which stay until deleted, and per series, a choice of episodes, stored as a count N ("download next": the latest in progress and the N unwatched after it, so the next is there before it ends, else the first N unwatched; specials left out) or `all` (specials too), kept however they're watched. An earlier `current` reads as the next 1.
* **Done with:** a series' download watched since it was fetched (its watched mark's clock past the fetch), unless wanted by hand or all of its series is chosen: deleted as its space is needed, oldest fetched first, only once another source holds it alike (its quality and size, not unavailable); the only copy, never. Reconciling also forgets copies whose files are gone (deleted by hand, or the folder changed), and never leaves less than 20 GB free.
* **Listings:** a crawl reads an `INDEX` if there is one (a path per line, as `find . -type f ! -name INDEX > INDEX` writes them, optionally followed by tab-separated size and modification time; a directory's ending in `/`, as Cinematheque's server lists them, so an empty one shows it's still there), else JSON listings (Caddy's or nginx's), else HTML, reading only links' targets.
* **Crawls:** breadth-first with concurrent workers (16 by default), reusing a previous crawl's listing of any directory with only files whose modification time hasn't changed, then bringing the source's copies in line with what was found. A crawl that fails anywhere changes nothing.
* **Unavailable:** a directory that held files and is still listed but holds none (an unplugged drive's mount point, or a NAS's) is one field on the source, `absent:<directory>` (relative to it, empty for the whole of it), noting when, its copies kept, rather than an operation per file; the topmost of an unavailable subtree only. A crawl clears it once the directory lists files again, or is gone from its parent's listing (deleted, its copies going as deleted files' do), or a year on, as if deleted; or a person removes its copies, as if deleted. Players show a title whose copies are all unavailable dimmed, and try unavailable copies last.
* **Serving:** a device listens on the port given, else on 8008 or, if that's taken, any free port, and advertises the port it got, binding with `SO_REUSEADDR` alone, never `SO_REUSEPORT`, so no two processes share a port. It answers `/sync`, and optionally serves media directories, each at a path of its own (the command-line app's one at `/media/`, the apps' by folder): files with single byte ranges, `If-Range`, and `mtime-size` ETags; listings as JSON (Caddy's form, on `Accept: application/json`) or HTML; and, for the command-line app's, an `INDEX` with sizes, times, and directories, built at start and again on a request once it's a minute old, so drives plugged in or unplugged show. The apps' folders have no `INDEX`, as they change; each is opened afresh for each request, and one that isn't there (a share unmounted) lists empty at its root, so a crawl finds it unavailable rather than failing.
* **Discovery:** devices advertise `_cinematheque._tcp` by DNS-SD (Bonjour on Apple platforms, Avahi's compatibility library on Linux), named for the device's key (`cinematheque-` and its first 8 hex digits), so a person's devices are told apart and a device skips only itself, with the library's ID, the sync path, the person's key, and the device's and person's names in the TXT record, so a device of the library whose person isn't a member yet can be shown, by name, to add; a new device offering to be added advertises `pair` (`new`), the pairing path, and its name instead.

## Command-line app

* **Commands:** [usage.txt](cli/src/usage.txt) lists them, and is their help. Devices join only by code (`person pair` or `library join` offering, `person pair CODE` adding the device as this person's, `member pair CODE` as a new member's); an offer prints its code and the port it listens on, for a device that can't find it nearby (`pair CODE URL`). State commands are `NOUN VERB`, with verbs `list`, `show`, `add`, `set`, and `remove` where they apply, over nouns `person`, `library`, `member`, `device`, `source`, `title`, `alias`, and `progress`; whole-app actions are single words (`crawl`, `sync`, `serve`, `refresh`). Options are named (`--port`, `--leave`); `ls` and `show` are shortcuts for `title list` and `title show`.
* **Usage:** placeholders are in capitals, named for what they mean rather than their type (`URL`, `--port PORT`). `-h` or `--help` shows usage, of one noun or action if given, as does a noun alone; a command line that isn't one shows the same and exits with status 2. A command that fails says why in words on stderr (`error: this device has no library: create one (library create), or join one (library join)`), saying what to do where there's something to do, never with a trace, and exits with status 1.
* **Downloads:** `title download` fetches a title's, a season's, or an item's files into a directory (the current one, or `--to`), as a device's downloads go (their paths, `.part` files, and only within the directory, since a crawling member's device chose the paths): in the quality given, else the one most of its items have; from its copies in copy racing's order, the next tried when one fails; a file already there at its size is kept.
* **Web client:** `serve --web` serves one at the root (plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, embedded in the app), off unless asked for, since anyone who can reach the port can use it; a browser acts as this device's person. It has the Mac's shelves (with Home), grid, tiles, and status line; title pages as the apps', of what browsers play (MP4, M4V, or MOV, HEVC only where supported); and plays as the apps do, full height with the browser's own controls, Back or Escape leaving. Its API under `/api/` is the apps' JSON (`views.zig`), with covers fetched by the server, since sources answer other sites' scripts nothing, and only URLs within the library's sources fetched or raced. A second after a browser's last change, it syncs with every device found or added. Its details are in [cli/README.md](cli/README.md#the-web-client-in-detail).
* **Added addresses:** addresses added are kept in `local`, on that device only; `sync` without a URL, and `refresh`, pull from every device discovered or added.

## Apple players

Their details, as built, are in [apple/README.md](apple/README.md#in-detail).

* **Structure:** one multiplatform app: a shared model, playback, title presentation, shelves, and setup, each platform's browser apart; one Xcode target, whose build phase links the core in as one object exporting only the C interface, so the compiler runtime Zig bundles can't clash with Xcode's; and, embedded on tvOS only, the Top Shelf extension.
* **Title presentation:** names as the core gives them; a quality picker by short names, the full name as help; the watchlist and watched marks quieter than what plays, the watched one showing its state, toggled by pressing. Start Over, before Resume, is the one way to start over, as Mark as Unwatched forgets the position too.
* **Shelves:** Home, Movies, Series, Watchlist, and Watched, with search, counts (a series' shelf counting its episodes too), and empty text; a tile's context menu has Add to Watchlist or Remove from Watchlist, and Mark as Watched and Mark as Unwatched (a season's on the Mac, a series' on the Apple TV, which shows a tile per series: removing every season of it listed, or adding the first not watched through). The Mac has In Progress in place of Home.
* **Setup:** Create a Library, or Join a Library, showing a code (a digit to a box, in two groups of three, as it's typed on the other device): on the Mac, in a welcome window as macOS apps' are (small and fixed, the app's icon and name over the two as large rows, the code shown in the same window, with Back); on the Apple TV, as tvOS's large choices. Neither app shows or takes keys.
* **Playing,** on every platform: fading in from black once the stream is ready (resuming 4 seconds before where it was left) as its sound rises, and out as it falls on leaving, the position saved there; saving every 10 seconds past the first 10 and where it's paused; marking an item watched at its end; going on to the next.
* **Copies:** a download on this Mac plays from its file, first. Else the chosen quality's available copies are raced (`cq_race`), so a server that's asleep costs a second or so, not a network timeout, and one that failed lately is tried last; when a copy doesn't load, the item's others follow, the quality's, then other qualities', the nearest at or below its resolution first, then the nearest above. An item lacking the chosen quality plays the first of those, which the Mac's inspector shows, dimmed, after its name, wrapping only whole ("Standard (720p Very Fast)").
* **Sharing:** while they run, the apps share the library (and the Mac its folders) on the port they got last, sync quietly a minute after they last did and whenever they come to the front, and a second after a change of their person's, pushing it to every device; they reload at once when a device pushes to them. Show sync activity shows the last exchange each way.
* **Settings' notes:** each says first why one would use the setting, then only what of how it works bears on choosing it (a NAS's disks waking, say); a confirmation says what an action does.
* **Retreat:** every flow has a way back out: a step's Back (or the platform's Back), a sheet's or dialog's Cancel, a download's Cancel; what can't be taken back is confirmed first, saying so.
* **Project:** Xcode's recommended settings but for script sandboxing (the core's build phase), the App Sandbox, the hardened runtime (until signing for distribution), and its macOS 15 minimum; a developer's signing settings in an ignored `Local.xcconfig`; `Info.plist` allowing plain HTTP and Bonjour on the local network.

## macOS player

* **Shelves:** Movies, Series, Watchlist, In Progress, Watched, and Downloaded (what this Mac keeps), in a sidebar; the one shown last is shown again at launch.
* **Grid:** covers in fixed cells, so a change in width reflows whole tiles and resizes none; movies 2:3, each series' seasons square under its name; marks for the watchlist, watched or progress, and downloads; a placeholder until a cover loads, replaced without a fade.
* **Keys and scrolling:** the arrow keys move the selection, scrolling just enough to show it; Return plays it, as double-clicking does. Each shelf keeps its place, by its topmost title, when shown again and as its width changes.
* **Inspector:** the selected tile's larger cover; a quality picker in a row of its own above Play (a title of one quality saying it as text, so every title says what it plays in), so choosing it changes only what's below, starting on the best of what Play plays; Play or Resume (Start Over before it) with the marks beside it; metadata; and a movie's versions or a season's episodes, each playable (an episode with the rest of the season queued), described on demand.
* **Downloading:** each in one row in which nothing appears or disappears (what doesn't apply is dimmed): a movie's Download (of several versions, the one Play plays, by name, "Download Extended", a version of parts all of them; the quality it comes in, dimmed, if not the one picked) with its size, by Control-click each quality under a Download heading (with its size, a warning symbol if it wouldn't fit, then the space free), or a season's Download pop-up of the series' choice (Nothing, Next, Next 3, Next 10, All, each with its size, the space free in its help); a gauge while it comes; Show in Finder, or Open in Browser if only servers hold it (its context menu listing every place that holds it, under what opening it does: the download, and each quality's folders on this Mac, under Show in Finder, and servers, their folder's listing, under Open in Browser, so none needs finding by hand); and a trash button, canceling or deleting (deleting what the series' choice asks for sets it to Nothing). A size is orange if it wouldn't fit. A version's, part's, or episode's row, and a movie tile, have the same in their context menus, its places and qualities flat, under Show in Finder, Open in Browser, and Download.
* **Crawling:** the grid fills in as a crawl commits, the tiles on screen described first.
* **Playing:** in the library's window, over the browser, Back or Escape returning to it, or with ⌘ held, in a window of its own; its title row shows only with the controls. Space, F, M, C (captions, in the languages preferred), and the arrows (10 seconds) work as in QuickTime Player; the display stays awake while it plays.
* **Settings:** four panes, by what people do (People, Devices, Sources, Downloads), addresses only under Advanced; names are ones everyone in the library sees; buttons with text have no symbols, as macOS's settings' don't. On launch the app names this Mac and you, if they have no names.
* **People:** members by name, with the founder, you renamable in place, and others removable, confirmed first (they come back only by joining again, as someone new); devices nearby of the library whose people aren't members yet, to Add; and Leave This Library…, as on the Apple TV, closing Settings for the welcome window once left.
* **Devices:** your devices, each renamable in place and, but for this Mac, removable; sharing the library on the network while the app runs; New Devices, each offering to be added, with boxes for its code, then Yours and Someone New, enabled once the code's in, each pairing it as whose it is, Yours then the default, which Return presses (someone else's device taking a click on Someone New, as giving yours to it would let it act as you; a wrong code shakes the boxes clear); under Advanced, devices by address and Show sync activity.
* **Sources:** Add Folder…, Add Server… (an address, as Finder's Connect to Server takes one), and Refresh Library; Look for videos under Advanced; each source with Show in Finder or Open in Browser, and Remove. Folders on this Mac are served while the app runs (one that isn't there shown as unavailable and served empty, so a crawl marks it unavailable), and moved with the app if it must serve on another port. A source's sheet has where its quality comes from (From File Names, From the Folder Under It, or Named), under Advanced, and its unavailable folders, each with Remove Videos.
* **Downloads:** the folder (`~/Movies/Cinematheque` unless chosen), served at `/downloads/`; what it holds and the space free; progress while downloading; and the rule for what goes.
* **Toolbar:** the sidebar's and the inspector's toggles (⌥⌘I), the shelf's title, and search; Refresh Library is ⌘R. At launch, with a library, the app shares it, syncs, names itself if need be, then crawls, syncing before writing anything, so a device whose replica was lost learns its own writes back first.
* **Status bar:** floating along the bottom of the grid, so fading moves nothing: the shelf's counts, or what went wrong; what's running, with progress, or the last exchanges if Show sync activity is on. It fades 3 seconds after it last changed, unless something is running or wrong, and comes back when the pointer nears it.
* **Tooltips:** short, sentence-style fragments without a period ("Delete download"), saying only what the control and the text around it don't: its name, for one that shows only a symbol; else a warning or state, as "The only copy". Explanations go in the pane's text.
* **Modelessness:** no alert nobody asked for, after the original Macintosh guidelines. An action is confirmed first only if it's hard to undo: removing a source (saying how many videos go, on every device, its files untouched), removing someone, leaving the library, and deleting a download that's the only copy. Removing a device isn't, as it can join again as easily. What goes wrong shows in the status bar (and along the bottom of Settings) in words, never the core's error names, each source by its name, until dismissed, until the next action of its kind succeeds, or until the device gains or leaves a library.

## Apple TV player

* **Data directory:** in the caches, the only place tvOS lets an app keep files, which it may purge; its seeds are kept in the Keychain too and put back at launch, so a purge loses only what syncing and crawling rebuild.
* **Refreshing:** at launch, naming itself (the Apple TV's name) if it has no name, sharing the library, syncing with devices found, then crawling the sources.
* **Exchanges:** with Show Sync Activity on, each exchange that changed something, in a corner for a few seconds.
* **Shelves:** a tab bar of Home (Continue Watching, then the watchlist), Movies, Series (a cover per series), Watchlist, Watched, and Settings and Search as symbols alone, so every tab fits; covers six to a row, as tvOS's lockups (the cover lifting as it's focused, its name moving out of its way), with their marks.
* **Settings:** as tvOS's Settings app lays its out (the app's icon in the left half, the focused row's explanation under it, which VoiceOver reads as the row's hint; the list in the right, a grouped form, its rows on plates of one height, their text inset as the system's, without symbols, under small uppercase headings; at accessibility text sizes, as the system's, values under their titles and the picture smaller): this Apple TV's name and its person's, each bringing up the full-screen keyboard as it's pressed (the system's Name page being private); Refresh Library; Look for New Videos, opening a page of its choices, titled at the top without the tab bar, its row's chevron the system's; Show Sync Activity; and Leave This Library…, confirmed first, or, on the founder's only device, asking who takes over. Sources and people are managed on the Mac.
* **Top Shelf:** Continue Watching, then the watchlist, as Home's rows (a dozen each), with covers; the app writes them, as they change, to its app group's container (`group.com.rbrian.cinematheque`), which the extension reads, and an item opens its title's page on Home (`cinematheque://title?tile=…`).
* **Pages:** a movie's or a series' (its seasons, the first not watched through outlined, specials last), over a blurred backdrop of its cover: Play or Resume, Start Over, a Quality picker after Play (a title of one quality saying it as text), and the marks, then versions or episodes (described as a season's shown); the quality the best it plays unless one's chosen: MP4, MOV, or M4V (not MKV or ISO), up to 2160p on a 4K display, else up to 1080p.
* **Playing:** full screen in the system's player, its picture fading in only once the display has switched to the video's frame rate and range (if Match Content is on) and it's playing, its sound rising; the remote and info panel work as the system player's.
* **Leaving:** Back fades it out as its sound falls, gives the display its own mode back behind black, then fades the page back in. Going to the background, it pauses, silent, and comes back paused.

## Debugging

* The apps log to the system's log (Xcode's console), under `com.rbrian.cinematheque`: playback's steps and their times (`playback`), and each crawl's per source (listing, listings read and reused, and recording) and describing's after (`crawl`), from `cq_crawl`'s results.
* Debug builds take launch options for driving the app unattended (in a VM by `open --env`; in the tvOS simulator by `simctl launch`, each prefixed `SIMCTL_CHILD_`): `CINEMATHEQUE_CODE_FILE` (a file each pairing code offered is written to, for a test to send from another device), `CINEMATHEQUE_SELECT` (a tile, e.g., `t/s/firefly/-#1`: selected on macOS, its title's page opened on tvOS), `CINEMATHEQUE_TAB` (tvOS: a tab, `movies`, `series`, ...; on both, in setup, a step, `join`, offering its code), `CINEMATHEQUE_DOWNLOAD_FLOOR` (the free space downloads keep, in bytes, for a VM's small disk; any build, read by the core), `CINEMATHEQUE_LEAVE` (tvOS, on Settings, once a launch: `ask` presses Leave This Library…, `confirm` confirms it too, the first other member taking over if asked), `CINEMATHEQUE_PLAY` (once a launch: a URL to play on macOS, with `CINEMATHEQUE_ITEM`, its item key, for its progress; any value on tvOS, to play the opened title).

## Implementation

* Zig 0.17+ for the core and the command-line app, with no dependencies beyond the system's SQLite and DNS-SD. Whatever waits (networking, sleeping, SQLite waiting out another connection's write, DNS-SD waiting for results) does so through Zig's `Io`, so it can be canceled; only what `Io` can't reach blocks outside it (SQLite's own disk I/O, and the system's name lookup, which copy racing avoids on Apple platforms by looking names up through DNS-SD).
* SwiftUI for Apple players.
* SQLite for each device's replica.
* Tests: the core's unit tests (`zig build test`; `zig build` compiles the C interface too), [cli/test.sh](cli/test.sh) (two devices and more, end to end), screenshots of the macOS player in a macOS VM (terrarium's `macos-27.2-quiet`), and of the Apple TV player in the tvOS simulator (an Apple TV 4K, 3rd generation).

## Scale

100,000+ video files: 4,000+ movies, and 500+ TV series of up to 30 seasons of up to 50 episodes.

## Risks

* App Review may resist publishing a library that encroaches on Apple's own, all the more so one that serves media.
* Outside metadata comes from providers outside Cinematheque, whose sources' terms (e.g., TMDB's charge for commercial use, IMDb's datasets for personal use only) bind them, not Cinematheque.
* GPL tools (`HandBrakeCLI`, GPL builds of FFmpeg) must run as separate processes, never linked, or they bind the license of the players and the shared core.
