# Cinematheque

Local-first video libraries. Players on Apple devices, macOS, Linux, and Windows browse shared libraries and play each video from any source that holds it. There is no central server: each library's titles, metadata, and people's data replicate among devices, so every device works offline and catches up when it reconnects.

This file specifies what's built. How operations are written, merged, and synced, and the keys and fields the library writes, are in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md); plans and open questions are in [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md); the measurements behind the design are in [experiments](experiments/README.md); outside specifications are indexed in [docs/specifications](docs/specifications/README.md).

## Lexicon

One word for each thing, in the spec, the code, the command-line app, and the apps alike; stored keys and fields keep their names as data. The apps show people only the words marked † (in everyday senses); the rest are for developers, and the apps say what they mean instead.

* **Source†:** anything serving videos over HTTP (a NAS, a web server, a folder a device shares); a device's downloads are one, though not listed. Not "repository."
* **Copy†:** a file of an item, in one quality, that a source holds (stored as `held:<source>`). Not "holding."
* **Device†:** a running Cinematheque with keys of its own (an app, the command-line app); the one it syncs with is the other device. Not "peer."
* **Person** (shown as people†): a human, with a key and devices; a member†, once admitted to a library. Not "user" (but for what's stored: the data directory's and the Keychain's `user` seed, and certificates' and names' `user` fields).
* **Progress:** where a person stopped in an item, and its watched mark. Not "playhead"; how far a long operation has come is its steps.
* **Unavailable†:** a folder that held files and lists none (stored as `absent:`). Not "absent," "missing," or "not found."
* **Pair†:** what a new device does to be another of a person's devices; to come in as a new member, it joins†. Not "link."
* **Data directory:** a device's own directory, of its keys and replica. Not "home," which is taken for `$HOME` (but for what's stored: the Keychain's `com.rbrian.cinematheque.home` items).
* **Share†:** what a device does with its library and folders (its server serves them).
* **Delete†** takes files off a disk; **remove†** takes something out of a list or the library (as an unavailable folder's videos); **forget†** discards only what's known (as an address added, or where a person stopped).
* **Keep†:** to spare a download deletion; **download next†:** a series' next episodes, as it's watched.
* **Quality†:** one encoding of an item (stored as a source's `rendition` field). Not "rendition." **Version†:** an edition (a director's cut); **origin:** a quality's Blu-ray, web, DVD, or HDTV token.
* **Sync** and **crawl**, shown as catching up† (with devices) and looking for new videos† (in sources), together Refresh Library†.

## Core interface

* **Threads:** its functions belong to one thread but for long ones (crawling, syncing, describing), each on a connection of its own; only one connection writes as the device at a time, and the main one catches up (`Replica.refresh`) before acting. Strings passed in are copied if kept.
* **Long operations** commit as they go; `cq_data_version` changes when they have, `cq_steps` tells how far one has come (its phase, and steps done of how many), and `cq_prioritize` names titles (e.g., those on screen) for a crawl to describe first.
* **Serving and syncing:** `cq_serve` serves the library (and folders, each at a path of its own) on a task of its own until `cq_stop_serving` or `cq_close`; `cq_served` tells of the last sync it served (to which device, how many operations), and `cq_received` of the last push it took (from which device, how many operations won); `cq_sync`'s results name each device as it advertised itself, and say how many operations were pushed to it.
* **Pairing:** `cq_pair_offer`, `cq_pair_check`, and `cq_pair_stop` offer a new device to pair, as another of a person's devices or to join, and `cq_pair` pairs one, returning its person's key and its own; `cq_name` names another device, as one just paired.
* **People and devices:** `cq_status` tells whether this device is paired as another of a person's devices. `cq_set_name`, `cq_devices`, `cq_nearby` (devices found nearby, with names, and addresses added), and `cq_device_address` (adding an address, or forgetting one) let apps show people and devices by name. `cq_remove_progress` removes this person's progress in an item, and `cq_remove_device` another device.
* **Downloads:** `cq_downloads_configure` sets where a device keeps downloads and the URL it serves them at, `cq_download` and `cq_download_next` say what to download, `cq_keep` keeps a download however it's watched, `cq_download_remove` deletes, `cq_downloads` tells all of it (what's held, and which are the only copies, wanted, next, kept, and watched since downloaded, free space, and how far reconciling has come), and `cq_reconcile` (long) fetches and deletes to match; while one runs, another asks it to run again and returns; `cq_downloads_hold` holds new downloads back while a stream plays.

## Devices

* **Players:** every client, each able to sync and crawl as well as play.
* **Refreshing:** every device syncs with the others first, then crawls: the catalog other devices have crawled and described arrives first, so the crawl finds little to do (a device's first crawl after syncing adds and describes nothing unchanged).
* **Sources:** anything that serves videos over HTTP.
* **Static servers:** sources running no Cinematheque code, such as a consumer-grade NAS or a web server (Caddy, nginx, Apache).
* **Desktop devices:** macOS, Linux, and Windows players. They are sources too, sharing their local and downloaded videos, and differ from static servers only in what they hold and how long they stay online.

## Identity and membership

* **Data directory:** the device's own directory: `~/Library/Application Support/Cinematheque` on macOS, `$XDG_DATA_HOME/cinematheque` (`~/.local/share/cinematheque`) elsewhere, or another by `--data` or `CINEMATHEQUE_DATA`, which the C interface, and so the app, honors too. It holds `identity` (the device's random seed), `user` (the person's seed, if paired as another's device), `library` (the founder's person key), and `replica.db`.
* **Keys:** a device's keys derive from its seed, and its person's from the person's seed, which the person's devices share; a device not paired as another's derives its person's seed from its own. Pairing a device as another of a person's means giving it the person's seed, which is a secret, and is done only by pairing, so the seed is never shown.
* **Pairing:** a new device, before it has a library, shows a 6-digit code and offers to pair, to be another of a member's devices or to join (as a new member), serving `/pair` and advertising `pair` (`pair` or `join`) by DNS-SD. A member's device, given the code, sends it the founder's key, and if pairing as theirs, the person's seed, sealed with a key both derive by CPace (ristretto255, SHA-512) from the code and the offer's kind, so an eavesdropper learns nothing, each attempt tests one guess, and the kind can't be changed on the way. The new device answers with its person's key and its own, sealed the same way: a joining one's person is added as a member, and the member's device names the new device, as it advertised itself (or, if that's a generic name such as tvOS gives apps, "Apple TV," as its host is named, `Not-Your-AppleTV.local` as "Not Your AppleTV"), under its person (the member's, if pairing as theirs), so it's listed among that person's devices at once, until it names itself. It stops after 3 wrong codes, until it offers again with a new one.
* **Certificates:** a device's first write is its certificate, its person's signature over the library and the device key. A device's private stream has a certificate of its own. Before writing, once a connection, a device checks its own certificates (its stream's, and its private stream's, which travels on its stream) and writes any missing or invalid one afresh, at the current time so it wins; the apps, on opening the library, rewrite one they hold that doesn't verify, without waiting to write (but never write a missing one first, since a device whose replica was lost must sync first): a device's own writes pass without one, so only other devices would notice, by refusing its whole stream (as one an older build certified wrongly).
* **Library:** its ID derives from its founder's person key. Members add people to a set; any member may remove another, except the current founder.
* **Handover:** the founder may hand the library over to a member, staying or leaving. Handovers are a numbered chain, each valid only if written by the previous generation's founder, so devices agree in any order.
* **Removal:** any member may remove a device, without its person, or a member, with every device of theirs it knows. Each device's streams (its own and its private one) are cut off where the remover had seen them (`revoked`, a field per stream): writes through there stand, later ones are refused, and devices serve it no more; a removed member's device the remover didn't know isn't admitted. A device that took later writes before hearing of the removal drops them, notes their fields, and repairs them from a device that refused them, fetching just those fields' winners with one shared proof per batch; a write only it held, which lost to a refused one, is lost. A removed device still holds its person's seed, so it could pair again as a new device; keeping it out for good would take a new person seed. A removed device is told so when it next syncs: the device refusing it signs a refusal (`removed`) over the library, the device, and its request's time, which the device believes only if a member certified the signer and it doesn't know the signer removed; it then forgets the library and its identity (`cq_leave`; on tvOS, the Keychain's seeds too), stops the refresh under way, and returns to setup, saying why, to pair again as a new device.

## Replication

* **Operations:** each writes one field of one key. Fields merge last-writer-wins by hybrid logical clock, ties broken by device key; set elements merge by causal length.
* **Batches:** a device signs each batch of up to 1,024 consecutive writes (a transaction's) once, with Ed25519, over a BLAKE3 Merkle tree of their encodings, so any operation, or set of them, is verifiable by proof.
* **Storage:** SQLite holds each field's winning operation (as columns, from which its encoding is rebuilt), interned device keys, signed batches, a version vector of each device's highest sequence number covered, fields awaiting repair, and state of the device's own (`local`, e.g., crawl snapshots). It runs in WAL mode, and connections wait up to 10 seconds for one another's writes, since the command-line app's `serve` and `refresh` share its replica.
* **Sync,** both ways, started by either side: a device POSTs to `/sync` a request signed by its device, with its certificate, the time, and its version vector. The other device answers members only, within ten minutes of its own clock, with frames: per device, its winning operations above the vector, grouped by batch, each batch with its signature and the leaves of operations not sent; then its vector, which the device adopts; and its own device key and certificate, in headers. The device then pushes what the other device lacks: a second POST to `/sync` (its own content type) of a signed request and the frames above the other device's vector, leaving out private streams not the other device's person's, which the other device applies as it would a response. So a device that can reach another, though that one can't reach it (behind a NAT), syncs both ways. Frames are gzipped; a device too old to take pushes refuses one, and is only pulled from.
* **Own private stream:** a device takes its own private stream's batches as its person's even before the certificate arrives, so a device whose replica was lost (as tvOS may purge one) gets its progress back from its person's other devices.
* **Admission:** a receiver applies a batch only if its device's certificate names a member, else holds it until the end of the response, then rejects it without claiming it in its vector. It refuses operations past a removed device's cutoff, handovers not from the previous founder, and private data not from its person's private stream.
* **Private data:** a person's progress (`p/<person>/...`) is written on each device's private stream, with its own key and numbering. Devices send private streams only to the devices of the person they're of, and leave them out of the vectors they send others, so private data travels only directly between a person's devices.

## Library

* **Keys:** `t/<m|s>/<normalized title>/<year or ->` for a title; below it `/v/<edition or ->` for a movie's version (its `edition` field naming it as written) or `/e/s03e17` for an episode (then `/v/<edition>` for an episode's version; `/e/s00-<normalized title>` for a special without a number, its `title` field naming it), `/p/<n>` for a part, and `/r/<quality>`, whose `held:<source>` field records the file's path, size, and modification time, empty when no longer held. A title's key derives from its parsed identity, so devices crawling independently agree.
* **Naming:** the parser reads Plex, Jellyfin, and Kodi conventions and download names: ID tags, years (the last year ends a title), editions, parts (after the year), extras, `S01E02`, `1x02`, air dates, season folders, and Kodi's compact codes (`Show 317 Title`) right after the series' title from the enclosing folder. Dots between digits are a title's own (`11.22.63`); a name of numbers alone without a year (e.g., a camera's) goes to review. A numberless file beside a series' episodes whose name extends the series' (`Babylon 5 The Gathering`), or in a `Specials` folder, is a special (season 0).
* **Qualities:** a file's quality is its source's, if named; else its name's technical tokens, canonically (resolution, origin, codec, dynamic range, e.g., `2160p remux hevc hdr`); else unknown (`-`). Two files of one quality in one source are numbered (`~2`) as duplicates.
* **Review:** among titles some source holds, close matches (a year off by one, missing, or with transposed digits; the same year, the same digits, and a spelling within two edits; or only diacritics differ) and duplicates. An alias (`aliases`) records that one title key is another's; it replicates, and an empty one undoes it.
* **Progress:** per person and item (version, episode, or part), above qualities: position and duration in milliseconds, and watched, set on reaching the last 5% or by hand. A position in the first 10 seconds isn't kept: recording one empties the position and duration (not the watched mark), so stopping at the start sticks. Removing progress empties all three.
* **Covers and metadata:** after each crawl, one file per movie and per season, across every source, describes it, unless the file that last did is unchanged; else the best (a sidecar, then an MP4, then another file; a movie's standard version before its editions). Describing runs after crawling, so titles show before covers, with 16 workers, prioritized titles first, committing every 24 jobs or 750 ms. A network failure is retried next crawl; a file that isn't a readable MP4 records an empty cover, so it isn't reread until it changes. The cover comes from a sidecar image by Plex, Jellyfin, and Kodi convention (`poster.jpg`, `folder.jpg`, `<movie>-poster.jpg`, `season01-poster.jpg`, a season folder's `poster.jpg`), else an MP4's, read by range requests that walk atom headers to `moov` and read `udta` whole (about 8 requests). The cover is recorded as a byte range of the file (`art`, or `art:s01`... for seasons, on the title's key), so players fetch it with one range request; images don't replicate. Each cover's placeholder replicates: `thumb` (or `thumb:s01`...), a ThumbHash in base64 of its JPEG's DC coefficients (an eighth-size image, decoded without a full decode), or `-` for another format; players render it with `cq_thumbhash`. A cover described without one gets one from a single range request. MP4 metadata fills a title's `description`, `genre`, `released`, and `network`, and an item's `title` (an episode's), `description`, and `duration`; other episodes are described on demand.
* **Tiles:** for browsing, a movie or a season (a season without a cover takes its series'), with this person's progress: watched if every item is, else the share of its running time played (watched items whole, so one stopped in its last 5% counts in full, and items partway to their positions; an item of unknown length takes the average of those known), with the milliseconds elapsed and the runtime; and whether any item has progress (begun), which puts a season with an episode partway on Home and Played.
* **Watchlist:** per person, private like progress (`p/<person>/watchlist`, a field per movie's title key, or per season, `<title key>#<season>`, each season listed on its own).
* **Names:** people's and devices' (`n/<key>`'s `name`, and a device's `user`, its person's key), set by any member, shown in place of keys.
* **Facets:** titles held by some source, counted by kind, decade, and quality.

## Sources

* **Settings:** a source is a URL (percent-encoded as typed), with an optional quality name and a prefix of names to skip (default `,`), changeable later (applied at the next crawl). Its ID is a hash of the URL. Removing it clears its settings and copies for every device, and the remover's crawl snapshot.
* **A device's downloads:** a source of its own, `managed` (a replicated field), which no device crawls: each file fetched is recorded as its copy in the very quality it came from, so devices see it and play from it. What a device keeps is its own (`local`): items wanted (a title or season wanted is its items as of then, each in the quality the player's picker shows), and per series, its next N episodes ("download next") after where its person is, specials aside: the latest in progress and the N unwatched after it, so the next is there before it ends, else the first N unwatched. While a stream plays on the device, no download begins (one under way finishes), so as not to slow it; the rest follow once none plays. Files go under their paths in the source they came from, into `<path>.part` resumed by a range request, never written past their size, and only within the folder. A download watched since it was fetched (its watched mark's clock past the fetch) is done with, unless kept: deleted, wanted no longer, only once another source holds it alike (its quality and size, not unavailable), at once if the device says so, else only as its space is needed, oldest fetched first; the only copy, never. Reconciling also forgets copies whose files are gone (deleted by hand, or the folder changed), and never leaves less than 20 GB free.
* **Listings:** a crawl reads an `INDEX` if there is one (a path per line, as `find . -type f ! -name INDEX > INDEX` writes them, optionally followed by tab-separated size and modification time; a directory's ending in `/`, as Cinematheque's server lists them, so an empty one shows it's still there), else JSON listings (Caddy's or nginx's), else HTML, reading only links' targets.
* **Crawls:** breadth-first with concurrent workers (16 by default), reusing a previous crawl's listing of any directory with only files whose modification time hasn't changed, then bringing the source's copies in line with what was found. A crawl that fails anywhere changes nothing. A directory that held files and is still listed but holds none (an unplugged drive's mount point, or a NAS's) is unavailable: one field on the source, `absent:<directory>` (relative to it, empty for the whole of it), noting when, its copies kept, rather than an operation per file; the topmost of an unavailable subtree only. A crawl clears it once the directory lists files again, or is gone from its parent's listing (deleted, its copies going as deleted files' do), or a year on, as if deleted; or a person removes its copies, as if deleted. Players show a title whose copies are all unavailable dimmed, and try unavailable copies last.
* **Serving:** a device listens on the port given, else on 8008 or, if that's taken, any free port, and advertises the port it got. It binds with `SO_REUSEADDR` alone, never `SO_REUSEPORT`, so no two processes share a port. It answers `/sync`, and optionally serves media directories, each at the root (the command-line app's one) or at a path of its own (the apps'): files with single byte ranges, `If-Range`, and `mtime-size` ETags; listings as JSON (Caddy's form, on `Accept: application/json`) or HTML; and, at the root, an `INDEX` with sizes and times, and directories (built at start, and again on a request once it's a minute old, so drives plugged in or unplugged show; the apps' changing folders have none, and crawls read their live listings; each of the apps' folders is opened afresh for each request, and one that isn't there, as a share unmounted, lists empty at its root, so a crawl finds it unavailable rather than failing).
* **Discovery:** devices advertise `_cinematheque._tcp` by DNS-SD (Bonjour on Apple platforms, Avahi's compatibility library on Linux), named for the device's key (`cinematheque-` and its first 8 hex digits), so a person's devices are told apart and a device skips only itself, with the library's ID, the sync path, the person's key, and the device's and person's names in the TXT record, so a device of the library whose person isn't a member yet can be shown, by name, to add; a new device offering to pair advertises `pair` (`pair` or `join`), the pairing path, and its name instead.

## Command-line app

* **Commands:** [usage.txt](cli/src/usage.txt) lists them, and is their help. Devices pair and join only by code; an offer prints its code and the port it listens on, for a device that can't find it nearby (`pair CODE URL`). State commands are `NOUN VERB`, with verbs `list`, `show`, `add`, `set`, and `remove` where they apply, over nouns `person`, `library`, `member`, `device`, `source`, `title`, `alias`, `progress`, and `agent`; whole-app actions are single words (`crawl`, `sync`, `serve`, `refresh`). Options are named (`--port`, `--leave`); `ls` and `show` are shortcuts for `title list` and `title show`.
* **Usage:** placeholders are in capitals, named for what they mean rather than their type (`URL`, `--port PORT`). `-h` or `--help` shows usage, of one noun or action if given, as does a noun alone; a command line that isn't one shows the same and exits with status 2.
* **Downloads:** `title download` fetches a title's, a season's, or an item's files into a directory (the current one, or `--to`), under their paths in the source that held them, so the directory, served and crawled, names them alike: in the quality given, else the one most of its items have; from its copies in copy racing's order, the next tried when one fails; into `<path>.part`, resumed by a range request, renamed once whole, and never written past the file's size; a file already there at its size is kept; and only paths within the directory (a crawling member's device chose them).
* **Added addresses:** addresses added are kept in `local`, on that device only; `sync` without a URL, and `refresh`, pull from every device discovered or added.
* **Background:** on macOS, `agent add` loads two launch agents (`com.rbrian.cinematheque.serve`, keeping `serve` running, and `.refresh`, syncing with devices discovered or added, then crawling, hourly), logging to `~/Library/Logs/Cinematheque`.

## Apple players

* **Structure:** one `App` and root view (its data directory unopened, setting up, or browsing; at launch and once set up, naming, sharing, and refreshing), over a shared model, cover cache, playback session, title presentation (facts, runtimes, episode names, Play labels, Start Over (`backward.end.fill`, labeled, before Resume, as a player's timeline starts at its leading edge, playing what it would from the beginning), the quality to play, and the watchlist and watched marks, the watched one showing its state, Watched or Unwatched, which pressing toggles), shelves (Home, Movies, Series, Watchlist, and Played, a smart folder of everything with progress, in progress or watched, from which Remove from Played, in a tile's context menu, removes its progress (a season's, on the Mac, as Remove from In Progress or Remove from Watched, and elsewhere Mark as Unwatched; a whole series', on the Apple TV, which shows one tile per series): their tiles, search, counts, and empty text; the Mac's sidebar shows all but Home, with Played split in two: In Progress, Home's, and Watched), and debug launch options, with setup (`SetupView`: one form on the Mac, tvOS-style steps on the Apple TV) shared too, and each platform's `Browser` and the rest apart (`#if os(...)`), and what else differs (the data directory, default names) in `Platform`, by one Xcode target (Xcode 27's `project.xcproj` format), whose build phase builds the core for the platform and each architecture and links it into one object exporting only the C interface, so the compiler runtime Zig bundles can't clash with Xcode's.
* **Setup:** pairing by a code this device shows (`OfferedCode`), to be another of the person's devices or to join someone's library, or creating a library. A code shows a digit to a box, in two groups of three, as it's typed on the other device. On the Mac, one form: Pair with Your Other Device and Ask to Join, each showing its code in place, and Create Library. On the Apple TV, as tvOS apps set up: the app's name over three large choices (I Use Cinematheque on Another Device, and Join Someone's Library, each a screen showing its code; Start a New Library). Neither app shows or takes keys; the command-line app still does.
* **Playing** (`FadingIn`), on every platform: fading to black while the stream warms up, paused (loaded, sought to its position, less 4 seconds when resuming, and prerolled), then playing and fading to the picture as its sound rises from silence, and when left, fading to black as its sound falls, its position saved where that fade ends, so resuming replays the 4 seconds before it; a curtain over both (`Curtain`), each fade 0.5 seconds (`Platform.fade`); a download on this Mac from its file, first and unraced; else streaming from the source: first from the copy whose server answers first (copy racing, `cq_race`, in the core, for every player), each copy's asked at once (`HEAD`, 1.5 seconds; one that didn't answer in the last 10 minutes, as this device notes, asked last), so a device that's asleep costs a second or so, not a network timeout; then trying the item's other copies in turn (the quality's, then its other qualities') when one doesn't load, as on a drive that's unplugged; starting at its position unless watched, saving it every 10 seconds past the first 10 and where it's paused or left (on the Mac, the first save reconciles downloads, so a kept series' next episode comes while this one plays), marking an item watched at its end, and going on to the next.
* **Sharing:** the apps share the library (and the Mac its folders) while they run, serving on the port they got last, so other devices sync with them directly; and they sync quietly (without showing it as an activity) a minute after they last synced, and whenever they come to the front (as an Apple TV app returns from the home screen, never relaunched), so what other devices change arrives without a refresh. After a change of their person's (pausing or leaving playback, a watched mark, the watchlist, Remove from Played), once changes stop for a second, they sync, which pushes the change to every device at once; and when a device pushes its changes to them, they reload at once. For diagnosis, each shows the last exchange each way that changed something (one with nothing new isn't told of) in one line: what it received, from which device, and what it sent, to which device, and when.
* The project follows Xcode's recommended settings but for script sandboxing (the core's build phase), the App Sandbox (the data directory it shares with the command-line app), the hardened runtime (until signing for distribution), and its macOS 15 minimum. A developer's signing settings stay in an ignored `Local.xcconfig`.
* The bundle's `Info.plist` allows plain HTTP and Bonjour on the local network.

## macOS player

* **Browsing:** shelves (Movies, Series, Watchlist, In Progress, Watched (what's watched through, and any with a download watched since), and Downloaded, what this Mac keeps; no All), the one shown last shown again at launch (at first, In Progress), searchable; a grid of covers in fixed cells (148 points wide at any width, so a change in width reflows whole tiles and resizes none), movies 2:3 and each series' seasons square under its name (each season's cover whole, fit and centered, here and in the inspector), each with a star at its top leading corner if on the watchlist, a watched mark (top trailing) or progress bar, and a download mark (bottom trailing) if this Mac holds any of it, or, if watched since, an hourglass (waiting for its space to be needed) or an orange mark (the only copy, for a person to delete or keep); a cover shows its placeholder until loaded (without one, a color with its title), then replaces it at once, without a fade. Each shelf has its own scroll view, and returns to its topmost title when shown again; the grid keeps that title at the top as its width changes (e.g., the inspector opening or closing) and its rows reflow, putting it back only when the columns change, not at every width an animation passes through. A title, not an offset, which a change in width would move; the topmost is the first at least half on screen. Covers are fetched by range request, scaled with ImageIO off the main thread, and cached in memory and in the app's caches.
* **Inspector:** for the selected tile, a larger cover, Play or Resume (a movie, or a season's episode in progress, the latest if several, else its next unwatched, with the rest of the season queued), a quality picker, a movie's progress (and each episode's, in its row) with Start from Beginning (`arrow.counterclockwise.circle.fill`) beside it, which forgets the position, not the watched mark, so Resume becomes Play, the watchlist and watched marks, metadata, a movie's versions, and a season's episodes, each playable, described on demand, under a heading with Restart (a small button, `backward.end.fill`, playing from the first episode, the rest queued, shown only when Play would start elsewhere); downloading: a movie's Download (then Downloading…, or Downloaded, and once watched, waiting or the only copy, with Keep, and a trash button), a season's Download menu (Download Season, Download Next Episodes: Off, 1, 3, 5, or 10, and Delete Season's Downloads), with how many are downloaded and how many next ones download, and each episode's mark and context menu (Download, Keep Download, Stop Keeping Download, Delete Download, saying so if it's the only copy); deleting any download that's the only copy asks first. Season 0 is Specials.
* **Crawling:** while a crawl runs, the grid reloads as it commits (polling `cq_data_version` each second), and the tiles on screen are described first.
* **Playing:** in the library's window, filling it over the browser (which keeps its place), with the toolbar hidden and the title bar transparent, its row (as QuickTime Player's) holding the window buttons, Back (or Escape, or the queue's end) returning to the library, its black fading away to it, and what's playing, centered (a movie's name, or a series' with the episode's code and name), over a faint gradient, the whole row showing only as the player's controls do, while the pointer moves and for 3 seconds after; with ⌘ held (⌘-click, ⌘-double-click), in a window of its own, titled with what's playing, as many as wanted. Either way, Space plays or pauses, F toggles full screen, M mutes, C toggles captions (in the languages preferred, the rest of the queue too; a beep if there are none), and the left and right arrows skip back and forward 10 seconds (the Apple TV's remote does as the system's player does), in an `AVQueuePlayer` in AppKit's `AVPlayerView`. Player windows aren't restored at launch.
* **Settings,** by what people do, with addresses under Advanced, in four tabs: People (members by name, with the founder, you renamable in place, and devices nearby of the library whose people aren't members yet, to Add); Devices (your named devices, this Mac among them, each renamable in place (tvOS tells apps only a generic name, Apple TV) and, but for this Mac, removable; sharing the library on the network while the app runs; New Devices, always shown, listing new devices nearby offering to pair, to be yours or to join, each with six boxes for the code it shows (in two groups of three, as the device shows it, so its length shows before it's typed; pasting works, and only digits go in), pairing it at the sixth digit (and adding a joining one's person; a wrong code shakes the boxes clear, as the login window does) and moving it, animated, into Your Devices (or People); under Advanced, devices by address, e.g., beyond a NAT); and Sources (Add Folder… and Add Server…, as buttons, not a menu, and Refresh Library, a button (`arrow.clockwise`, Library); each source's row ending in its own Show in Finder (a folder on this Mac) or Open in Browser (a server, its listing) and Remove (a minus), as in its context menu, confirmed first; folders on this Mac, served at `http://<Mac>.local:<port>/<folder>/` while the app runs and kept in the app's settings (one that isn't there, on a drive unplugged or a share unmounted, say, is shown as unavailable, and served empty, so a crawl marks it unavailable and its titles show dimmed until it's back), and network addresses; readable names, e.g., "art › Movies › 2160p"; a quality and names to skip under Advanced; a source's unavailable folders, each with when it went and Remove Videos (a minus); devices' downloads not listed); and Downloads (the folder, `~/Movies/Cinematheque` unless chosen, as Apple's TV app keeps `~/Movies/TV`, served at `/downloads/`; what it holds and the space free; progress while downloading; and whether watched downloads go at once). Names are ones everyone in the library sees. On launch the app names this Mac and you (the computer's name, the account's first name) if they have no names. If it must serve on another port, it moves its folders' sources there. Long operations run off the main thread.
* **Toolbar:** the sidebar's toggle and the shelf's title at the leading edge; the inspector's toggle (⌥⌘I) and search at the trailing. Refresh Library (⌘R, in the View menu, and a button in Sources) syncs, then crawls, its steps showing as "Catching up with 1 of 2 devices…" and "Looking in 3 of 6 sources…"; at launch, if there's a library, the app shares it, syncs, names itself if need be, then crawls; syncing before writing anything, so a device whose replica was lost learns its own writes back first.
* **Status bar:** along the bottom of the grid, floating over it: the shelf's counts ("4,000 movies"; "12 of 4,000 movies match …" while searching), or what went wrong, at the leading edge; what a long operation is doing and how far it has come, with a progress bar, at the trailing, or when none is, the last exchange with devices each way that changed something ("Received 3 changes from firefly 1m ago · Sent 12 changes to Den just now", counting operations), unless a problem shows. It fades out 3 seconds after it last changed, unless something is running or wrong, and comes back when the pointer nears it; floating, so fading moves nothing.
* **Modelessness:** no spontaneous alerts, after the original Macintosh guidelines: like stealing focus, an alert nobody asked for interrupts. An action a person takes may ask to be confirmed if it's hard to undo: removing a source asks first (saying how many videos go, on every device, its files untouched), since only adding it again and looking for its videos anew undoes it; and deleting a download that's the only copy, which nothing undoes. What goes wrong shows in the status bar (and in a bar along the bottom of Settings), in words (never the core's error names), each source by its name in Settings and the core's error said plainly (e.g., "Looking for new videos failed for art › Movies › 2160p (and 5 more): the server has nothing at that address."), until dismissed, until the next action of its kind (refreshing, changing the library, reading it) succeeds, or until the device gains or leaves a library.

## Apple TV player

* **Data directory:** in the caches (the core's default on tvOS), the only place tvOS lets an app keep files, which it may purge; the seeds (`identity`, `user`, `library`) are kept in the Keychain too, and put back at launch, so a purge loses only what syncing and crawling rebuild.
* **Refreshing:** at launch, naming itself (the Apple TV's name) if it has no name, sharing the library (so its progress and watchlist reach the person's other devices), then syncing with devices found by DNS-SD, then crawling the sources itself.
* **Exchanges:** the last exchange with devices each way that changed something, shown for a few seconds in the corner after each.
* **Shelves:** a tab bar of Home (Continue Watching, then the watchlist), Movies, Series (a cover per series, its progress over every season's running time), Watchlist, Played, and Search; covers six to a row, as cards, with progress and watched marks, and a star if on the watchlist (a series', if any season is).
* **Pages:** a movie's, over a blurred backdrop of its cover: Play or Resume, the watchlist and watched marks, versions, and its description; a series': its seasons (the first not watched through, specials last, outlined; each cover whole, fit and centered in its square), Play for the season's next episode (before Resume, Start Over, on both pages), and its episodes, described as the season's shown.
* **Playing:** full screen in `AVPlayerViewController`: the page (and the tab bar) fades to black while the stream warms up, then the player is presented over it by UIKit (AVKit's own views don't belong in SwiftUI's), unanimated (black over black, were it animated), still paused while the display switches to the video's frame rate and range (asked for by the app, not the player, if Match Content is on), which blanks the screen; then it plays, its picture fading in as its sound rises. Menu (Back) fades it to black as its sound falls before it goes, over the black page, which then fades back in, and gives the display back its own mode; its info panel (and Now Playing) showing each item's title, description, and cover, on the shared playback session; started once a request (fading the page re-hosts it, which would restart a view's task), a superseded start stopping at once; the quality is the best it plays: MP4, MOV, or M4V (not MKV or ISO), up to 2160p on a 4K display, else up to 1080p.

## Debugging

* Debug builds take launch options for driving the app unattended (in a VM by `open --env`; in the tvOS simulator by `simctl launch`, each prefixed `SIMCTL_CHILD_`): `CINEMATHEQUE_CODE_FILE` (a file each pairing code offered is written to, for a test to send from another device), `CINEMATHEQUE_SELECT` (a tile, e.g., `t/s/firefly/-#1`: selected on macOS, its title's page opened on tvOS), `CINEMATHEQUE_TAB` (tvOS: a tab, `movies`, `series`, ...; on both, in setup, a step, `pair` or `joinOther`, offering its code), `CINEMATHEQUE_DOWNLOAD_FLOOR` (the free space downloads keep, in bytes, for a VM's small disk; any build, read by the core), `CINEMATHEQUE_PLAY` (once a launch: a URL to play on macOS, with `CINEMATHEQUE_ITEM`, its item key, for its progress; any value on tvOS, to play the opened title).

## Implementation

* Zig 0.17+ for the core and the command-line app, with no dependencies beyond the system's SQLite and DNS-SD. Whatever waits (networking, sleeping, SQLite waiting out another connection's write, DNS-SD waiting for results) does so through Zig's `Io`, so it can be canceled; only what `Io` can't reach blocks outside it (SQLite's own disk I/O, the system's name lookup).
* SwiftUI for Apple players.
* SQLite for each device's replica.
* Tests: the core's unit tests (`zig build test`), [cli/test.sh](cli/test.sh) (two devices and more, end to end), screenshots of the macOS player in a macOS VM (terrarium's `macos-27.2-quiet`), and of the Apple TV player in the tvOS simulator (an Apple TV 4K, 3rd generation).

## Scale

100,000+ video files: 4,000+ movies, and 500+ TV series of up to 30 seasons of up to 50 episodes.

## Risks

* App Review may resist publishing a library that encroaches on Apple's own, all the more so one that serves media.
* Outside metadata comes from providers outside Cinematheque, whose sources' terms (e.g., TMDB's charge for commercial use, IMDb's datasets for personal use only) bind them, not Cinematheque.
* GPL tools (`HandBrakeCLI`, GPL builds of FFmpeg) must run as separate processes, never linked, or they bind the license of the players and the shared core.
