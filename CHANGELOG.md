# Changelog

All notable changes to Cinematheque are recorded here.

## Unreleased

* Lists can hold titles not in the library yet: on the Mac, a list's Add Missing Title… (in the web client, the ＋ beside its name) puts a movie or a series' season on it by name and year, with a note such as an article's address. It shows dimmed, "Not in library", and becomes the real title once a file named alike arrives.
* In the web client, going back to a shelf, as from a title's page, puts it back as it was left, at once, where it was scrolled and its series still open, redrawing only the tiles that changed meanwhile (a season marked watched).
* The web client's Series shelf shows a tile per series, as the Mac's does, opening its seasons in place on a band below its row; Group by Series, beside the order, turns it off. A dated series' seasons are named by their years.
* An episode retagged in place gets its new title and description, read again as it's shown, as a season's cover already was once its file changed.
* On the Mac, View › Refresh Library Fully (⌘⌥R, shown with ⌥ held, or Option-clicking Refresh Library in Settings) lists every folder afresh, so files retagged in place, which a plain refresh misses as their folders' times don't change, get their new covers and descriptions; the command-line app's `crawl --full` does the same.
* A dated series' seasons (a YouTube channel's, by `tools/youtube.sh`) are named by their years, "2026", not "Season 2026"; on the Mac its tile says "2024–2026".
* **Security:** the Mac's Settings › People no longer offers to Add devices nearby that say they want to join: anyone on the network could advertise as someone you know and be let in with one click. People join only by code, entered in Settings › Devices as Someone New.
* **Security:** a device takes what another says it holds only from a member's device, and never for itself, so a stranger on the network answering a sync can no longer have a device skip others' changes, or stop it syncing for good. A member can no longer claim another person's private stream (and be sent their progress and watchlist), write a change dated in the far future that nothing could overwrite, make someone impossible to remove, take back a handover, or sign two batches over the same changes. A person removed from the library stays removed: they come back by joining again, as someone new.
* **Security:** another member can no longer point a source at another address, take it over, or mark it as their downloads so no device crawls it.
* **Security:** a device offering to be added takes 3 wrong codes from each address and 30 in all before it stops, so no one host on the network can end its offer by guessing.
* **Security:** a host on your network can no longer tie up a device by answering slowly, or by saying nothing: a request's head must arrive within 30 seconds, one address may hold at most 32 of a device's connections, and listings, covers, and syncs give up after 2, 1, and 10 minutes, and a download once a minute passes without a mebibyte.
* **Security:** smaller fixes: a device's served folders no longer serve or list hidden files (a shared home's `.ssh`); a person's devices count only by certificate or a name their own device wrote, not another member's say; data directories, seeds, and replicas are made their owner's alone on opening, as older builds may have left them readable; the Apple TV's seeds stay in its Keychain only, never in a backup restored elsewhere; the command-line app shows control characters in names as `?`, so a file's name can't act on the terminal; and `tools/youtube.sh` reads a URL starting with `-` as a URL.
* **Security:** only the founder can remove the founder's devices (another member could have had the founder's only device forget the founder for good), a removal can no longer be undone by another member, and a download is deleted for space only once the other copy's server says it has it too, not merely another member's record.
* **Security:** a device on your network can no longer hold up another's writes: a sync's response is read whole before it's applied, so one sent slowly no longer holds the library's write lock, and advertisements made up by the hundred no longer stall syncing for minutes (devices are looked for, at most 64 and for 4 seconds, before writing begins).
* Every device tells what build it runs at `/version` (what it was built from, and how), as `cinematheque --version` prints it and the Mac's About shows it.
* On the Apple TV, a series' cover shows whole, in its own shape (square, or 4:3), rather than cropped to a movie poster's.
* On the Mac, the arrow keys move the selection over the covers again.
* On the Mac, the Series shelf shows a tile per series; selecting one opens its seasons in place, on a band below its row, the series still selected and shown whole in the inspector, the series after it moving out of its way. View › Group by Series turns it off, for every season under its series' name.
* On the Apple TV, seasons are shown as on the Mac, so lists show just the seasons they hold; a series' tile, in Series and Search, opens a grid of its seasons, and a season's page moves to the seasons before and after it.
* On the Apple TV, naming a list brings up the keyboard at once, and deleting a list from its page goes back to Lists.
* On the Apple TV, the Lists tab heads each list's row with its name, with a small See All › beside it, opening the whole list as a grid; a list is renamed or deleted from its page, and New List comes after the last list.
* Named lists: Favorites, or any others, beside the Watchlist, which becomes a list like any other. Make, rename, and delete them on the Mac (in the sidebar), the Apple TV, and in the web client (a Lists tab); put a title on one from its page's Lists menu or a tile's Add to List. Home shows a row per list.
* A movie's or show's file retagged beside its poster (a sort name added, say) is read again; and the first crawl after updating reads every title once more, to find sort names.
* Date Added orders titles by when they came: a new title by when a crawl found it, one already in the library by its files' earliest modification time. Date Modified, new, orders them by their files' latest.
* Shelves sort by title, release date, date added, or date watched, either way: on the Mac by View › Sort By, in the web client by a menu beside search, each shelf keeping its choice. Titles sort without a leading "The", "A", or "An", or as an MP4's sort name says; what's being watched (In Progress, Watched, Continue Watching) sorts by when last watched.
* On the Mac, the player's Back is a round chevron button, as Apple's apps have beside a window's buttons.
* On the Mac, dragging the grid's scroll bar with the inspector hidden scrolls, rather than opening the inspector; the bar stays at the window's edge.
* On the Mac, the inspector runs the window's full height, its toggle before search while it's hidden and following its edge while it's shown, so dragging the divider can no longer put it under the toggle and take its clicks. Its shortcut is now ⌃⌘I.
* On the Mac, the scroll bar holds still while paging through a shelf, rather than stepping back and jumping when let go, and paging through Series no longer speeds up and slows down with each series' seasons.
* On the Mac, a movie's context menu comes up at once in a large library, rather than after a second.
* Canceling a download (on the Mac) or a keep (in the web client) stops the file under way, rather than letting it finish and keeping it.
* The web client's first load no longer hangs for up to a minute in Safari, which tries HTTPS first: the server closes such a connection at once, so Safari falls back to HTTP.
* The web client downloads: Download brings an episode or movie to the browser's own computer, through the server; and with `serve --downloads DIR`, Keep has the server keep titles (a movie, a season's next episodes, an episode), as the Mac app downloads to the Mac, for other devices to play from, with a Kept shelf.
* The web client no longer stalls while a NAS's disks wake: covers load three at a time, nearest the screen first, so pages open meanwhile.
* The web client's title pages put Quality above Play, as the Mac's inspector does, and keep Play and the marks on one row; its shelves' links stay on one line.
* The Mac app opens with the covers taking the keys, rather than search; Find (⌘F) goes to search.
* The Apple TV's Settings adds people and your devices (by the code a new device shows), and servers as sources, and removes them, as the Mac's do; adding one can be canceled.
* Refreshing no longer makes the apps sluggish while they look for new videos: the Apple TV's, especially, reloaded the whole catalog on its main thread every second. The catalog is read off the main thread, and in about half the time.
* The Mac app and the command-line app's `serve`, sharing a data directory, no longer try to sync with each other (they're one device), which could show "Catching up failed"; and a device that refuses a sync now says why, as when two devices' clocks are more than ten minutes apart.
* **Security, breaking:** syncs are sealed: only the two devices syncing can read what they send, so anyone listening on your network no longer sees your library, your progress, or your watchlist go by.
* **Security, breaking:** a sync request is now for one device alone, which a device asks for first, and a repair's fields are signed with it, so a host on the network posing as a device of your library can no longer replay a request it was sent to your other devices to read your library and your progress. Devices on older builds can't sync with these, and say so ("that device refused"): update them together.
* **Security:** another member can no longer have your downloads deleted, or a file of theirs written over one of yours: a device deletes and keeps only the downloads it recorded itself, downloads only to a video's path hidden nowhere, and never replaces a file of another size that isn't its own download (`title download` too, so a member's path can't overwrite, say, `.ssh/authorized_keys`). A size near 2^64 no longer overflows the space check. On the Mac, leaving the library no longer races its own syncing.
* **Security:** the web client (`serve --web`) answers only its own page: another site can no longer read your library and watch history through it (by rebinding its own name to your computer), or change your progress or watchlist. Open it by address or `.local` name. And a request asking `Expect: 100-continue`, as curl sends for a large body, no longer crashes a device's server.
* **Security:** a source's address must be a web address (http or https): one another member wrote otherwise is ignored, so it can no longer have your devices send requests of its making on your network, or the Mac's Open in Browser open a file, a share, or another app. Crawls follow redirects only within a source's server; the web client's cover proxy no longer reads past what it fetched, and fetches only within a source (not, say, `http://nas.evil.com` for `http://nas`).
* **Security:** one file on a server can no longer stop a device syncing for good (a name or MP4 description too long for other devices to take), blank the apps' library on every device (a name that isn't UTF-8, as on old shares, now shows with a replacement character), crash a title's page (a film named "R"), or hold a device's crawl indefinitely (a JPEG of endless scans, an MP4 of endless atoms, a listing of millions of folders). Encoded `..` and control characters in a server's HTML listings are refused, as they were in its other listings.
* **Security:** the core is built with Zig's safety checks on (ReleaseSafe), so a bug in reading what a server or another device sends stops the app rather than corrupting its memory. Build the command-line app with `zig build --release=safe`.
* The Apple TV's Top Shelf shows Continue Watching, then your watchlist, when Cinematheque is in the top row of the Home Screen; choosing a title opens its page. A title without a cover shows as it does in the app, and leaving the library clears the Top Shelf.
* A source can take its videos' quality from the folders just under it (Movies/HQ 1080p30 Surround/Alien.m4v), so one source holds every quality: in the Mac's source sheet, under Advanced, Quality: From the Folder Under It, or `source set --quality-by folder`. And a second copy of one quality reads "1080p 2" (or "Other 2"), not "-~2".
* Play picks the same episode on every device: the one in progress, else the first unwatched after the last you watched, so one you skipped isn't played again. The core decides it, as it now also orders episodes and ranks qualities for every player.
* The Mac has Leave This Library…, in Settings › People, as the Apple TV has, returning it to its welcome window; and removing someone from the library asks first, as they can come back only by joining again.
* On the Mac, the first run is a welcome window, as macOS apps have: the app's icon and name over Create a Library and Join a Library, the code shown in the same window. On the Apple TV, Create a Library comes first.
* The command-line app says what went wrong in words, and what to do about it, rather than printing an error's name and a stack trace.
* On the Mac, Control-click Show in Finder (or use Show Files in a context menu) for every place that holds a title: its download, each quality's folder on the Mac, and each server's folder, opened in the browser at its listing.
* Setting up asks one thing: Join a Library (showing a code) or create one. Whoever enters the code, in Settings > Devices, chooses whether the new device is theirs or someone new's. Devices on the previous version still pair this way, as theirs; one offering to join the old way must offer again.
* The core names what players show (episodes' labels, a list's names, qualities' short names), so the Mac, the Apple TV, and the web client name them alike.
* The Apple TV's Played tab is Watched, as on the Mac and in the web client: what's in progress is on Home.
* One way to start over: Start Over, before Resume, now on the Mac and in the web client too, and Mark as Unwatched, which now forgets where you stopped as well. The ↺ buttons beside progress meters, and Remove from Played, In Progress, or Watched, are gone; a tile's context menu has Mark as Watched and Mark as Unwatched.
* Downloads follow one rule: what you download yourself stays until you delete it, and a series' episodes you've watched go when their space is needed (unless you chose All, or it's the only copy). Keep Download, "Delete watched downloads right away", and the hourglass and orange marks are gone, and a series' Download menu offers Nothing, Next, Next 3, Next 10, and All. An earlier choice of Current becomes Next; Unwatched and a season's unwatched become Nothing, so choose again.
* What your devices last sent and received ("Received 3 changes from firefly") shows only with Show sync activity on, in Settings › Devices › Advanced on the Mac and in Settings on the Apple TV.
* Refresh Library says only that it's looking for new videos: catching up with your other devices, which it still does first, goes on by itself anyway.
* On the Mac, Settings › Sources is plainer: Look for videos moves under Advanced, and a source's sheet no longer asks for names to skip (the command-line app still sets them).
* The command-line app's `agent` command is gone: macOS doesn't let a launch agent reach the local network, so its agents couldn't reach a NAS. Run `serve` and `refresh --jitter 600` some other way (systemd and a timer on Linux, Terminal on macOS).
* Wording follows Apple's style guide more closely: "Finder" without "the", menu paths as Settings > Devices, Delete Only Copy… in place of a parenthetical, one style of tooltip (short, without a period), and "Finding covers…" while covers are found.
* On the Mac, Show in Finder, beside the download trash button and in context menus, selects a movie's or season's files on the Mac, downloaded or in its folders.
* Shows named by air date (`The Daily Show 2026.11.16`) now appear, a season per year with episodes in date order, labeled by date ("Play March 12"); before, they were listed by the command-line app but had no tile in the apps. [tools/youtube.sh](tools/youtube.sh) fetches a YouTube channel into such a folder, each file named with its video's title.
* Look for New Videos, in Settings on the Mac and the Apple TV (and `library set --crawl` in the command-line app), chooses how often your devices look in the library's servers on their own: Manually, Every Hour, Every 3 Hours, Every Day (the default), or Every Week, for everyone in the library, sparing a NAS's disks. A device that opens skips a server another crawled within that time, since syncing already brought what it found; Refresh Library still looks everywhere, whenever you know there's something new. The hourly refresh also starts at a random point in its first 10 minutes, so computers don't sync together.
* Checking downloads no longer records every one afresh each time, which wrote (and synced to every device) a change per download and made the Mac reload its whole library, a second's pause after a download setting changed or an episode was watched.
* On the Mac, Settings' "Delete watched downloads right away" responds at once, rather than waiting for a refresh or sync under way.
* On the Mac, a download shows how far it's come in a round gauge, in place of a spinner: a movie's, a season's (of the episodes asked for), and an episode's beside it.
* The Apple TV has Settings, a gear in its tab bar: rename it and yourself, refresh the library, or leave it, to pair again as another's device or join another library. Leaving removes the Apple TV from the library so your other devices stop listing it; if it's the founder's only device, it first asks who takes over.
* With VoiceOver on the Apple TV, each row of Settings says what it does, as the text under the picture does for a row as it's focused.
* The Mac inspector's Restart button is gone: the first episode's own Play button plays the season from its start, and Start from Beginning starts an episode over.
* Title pages are quieter: Play and Start Over have a row of their own, with the watchlist and watched marks set back at its end, and the Quality picker sits in the heading of the episodes or versions it plays, naming each quality briefly ("1080p", "4K HDR", "720p Very Fast") with its full name as help; in the web client, a Season pop-up replaces the row of season buttons.
* A web client: `cinematheque serve --web` offers the library at the root of its port, to browse (Home, Movies, Series, Watchlist, Played, search) and play in a browser, resuming and saving progress as the device's person, which reaches their other devices within seconds. The command-line app's `--media` directory moves to `/media/`, so add it as `http://<host>:8008/media/`.
* The Mac shows download sizes: a movie's Download button says its size, and the season's Download menu gives each choice's size and the space available for downloads (the disk's free space, less the 20 GB Cinematheque keeps free, as Settings › Downloads shows both), warning when it wouldn't fit; and a Current choice downloads only the episode you're watching.
* The Mac's movie download controls stay put: Download, a spinner while it comes, and a trash button that cancels or deletes, each dimmed when it doesn't apply; Keep Download moves to the movie's context menu, with the rest of its download items.
* The Mac's season Download menu is a pop-up of the series' choice, which only you change: Nothing, Next, Next 3, 5, or 10, Unwatched in a season, Unwatched, or All (kept even once watched); beside it, a spinner while episodes come and a trash button (dimmed when there's nothing to delete) to delete the season's downloads; deleting what the choice asks for sets it to Nothing, so they don't download again.
* A change (the watchlist, a watched mark, leaving playback) reaches other devices about 2 seconds sooner: its sync goes to the devices already found, without looking for them again.
* A change made while the app was syncing or refreshing (a watchlist addition, say) now reaches other devices once that's done, not up to a minute later.
* On the Apple TV, a video left for the Home Screen comes back paused, rather than playing at once at full volume; pressing Play brings its sound up.
* On the Mac, the season Download menu no longer lists Download Nothing twice when no next episodes are set; open inspectors and title pages show what a crawl or another device changed (a description, a new quality or copy), not only after a change of yours; a video that can't load shows AVKit's message, not a black window; a hung share no longer stalls the app while it checks folders; and the Series shelf counts an episode's versions as one episode.
* A repair request that names a field more than once, or a sync that fails partway after learning of a new device, no longer crashes a device or mixes up its devices' numbers; a certificate check that fails is retried, not skipped for the connection; and a stop request ends a server even while it can't accept.
* Reading a cover or an episode's length no longer overflows or allocates without bound on a file with absurd sizes, and a busy or waking server (503, 429, a timeout) no longer makes a cover or duration be recorded as missing: it's retried next crawl. Seasons numbered by year (`S2024E05`) get their own tiles.
* Crawls ignore a damaged snapshot instead of failing every time, refuse listings over 64 MiB or deeper than 64 levels, skip empty, `.`, and `..` names, and fall back to the other listing formats when a server answers 403 or 405 for a missing `INDEX`. Paths over 16 folders deep keep their file name.
* A resumed download is refused if the server answers from another range, and downloads keep 20 GB free on Linux too.
* Syncs and repairs bound what a frame may claim (a value, a batch, a version vector), so a decompression bomb is refused; a device no longer sends a private stream whose certificate it doesn't hold to another person; a connection learns of devices another added, instead of crashing; `writePrivate` refuses an empty or oversized batch; a found device's odd advertised path falls back to `/sync`; pairing keeps a recent attempt when asked again, so a loop of requests can't prevent it; and a server refuses connections beyond 256 at once.
* On the Apple TV, Menu pressed while a video warms up no longer fights the exit, and a cancelled start no longer leaves the page black. Playback fades no longer overlap, a player that fails to load no longer raises the curtain, and a watched episode no longer replays after copy racing.
* The apps skip rereading and redrawing the library when nothing changed; leaving a library clears its folders, downloads, and exchanges; covers' memory is bounded by size, failed covers aren't re-requested at once, and the disk cache is pruned (60 days, 500 MB).
* In the command-line app, `ls -h` and `show -h` show only their own command, the pairing offer advertises the computer's host name, `agent add` retries `launchctl bootstrap` and no longer throttles the serve agent, and `cli/test.sh` refuses to clear a directory it didn't make.
* On the Mac, a season's Download menu is one level: Download Nothing, Download Next Episode, Download Next 3, 5, or 10 Episodes (one checked, which its button says), a divider, then the actions Download Season {n} and Delete Season {n} Episodes (n the season's number); and "{n} of {N} downloaded" follows the button, N the episodes asked for (the season's, or its series' next ones), until they all have.
* On the Mac, the Series shelf's status bar counts episodes too ("300 series, 1,200 seasons, 15,000 episodes"), and In Progress's says "titles", as the other shelves' do, not "titles in progress".
* A shared folder with a dangling link, or a file dated before 1970, no longer fails its listing (and with it every crawl of the source); a connection that fails to arrive no longer stops a device serving; a slow client of `INDEX` no longer holds up others; and a device or person's name too long for DNS-SD is cut, not left unadvertised.
* A device's clock is taken up from its own latest writes when it opens its replica or catches up with another connection's, so a write after a restart, or after the wall clock stepped back, can no longer lose to the device's own earlier one.
* A device that took a removed device's or member's later writes before hearing of the removal now gets back the fields it dropped, asking each device it syncs with after pulling (repair existed in the core but nothing asked for it).
* **Breaking:** a batch's signature now covers its range (so a signed batch can't be presented as another), and a sync's frames end with the sender's signature over its request and their headers (so a host that only copied a device's certificate can't make a device adopt its vector, receive its person's private data, or drop a batch on the way). Devices on older builds can't sync with these, and must be updated together.
* A write that fails partway (a busy database, say) no longer leaves a sequence number with no signed batch, which would have stopped other devices from syncing with this one; and an aborted sync no longer leaves later writes unsigned.
* A device's seeds are readable by its owner only, and an unreadable identity file (rather than a missing one) no longer makes a new identity.
* A file name ending in an unclosed year (`Alien (1979`), a JPEG cover with out-of-range Huffman table numbers, and a sync response with out-of-range sequence numbers no longer crash the app.
* A path another member's device recorded for a download can't reach outside the downloads folder; `crawl --workers 0` (or a non-number) is a usage error, not an empty crawl, and a bad `--port` exits with status 2.
* On the Apple TV, playing an episode's version shows its season's cover and its description in the info panel.
* In the Mac app, the arrow keys move the selection over the covers, and Return plays it.
* The Apple TV's movie and season pages have a Quality picker, when there's a choice, as the Mac's inspector does.
* Playing races only the chosen quality's copies, so a quicker server never swaps in another quality (as a Mac's share of a NAS's 720p folder over Wi-Fi did for its 1080p), and a quality with one copy isn't raced at all.
* A copy on a server that's slow to answer at first (a NAS waking) costs a quarter of a second, not a second and a half, when another copy has answered.
* The Apple TV's first play of a launch starts smoothly: no spinner over the black, its sound rising with the picture once it plays, its servers' names already looked up, and no stutter as the page fades.
* On the Apple TV's Home, moving down from the tab bar goes to Continue Watching, not past it to a cover in the watchlist.
* On the Mac, the display no longer dims or sleeps while a video plays.
* On the Apple TV, Start Over is labeled, with the to-the-beginning symbol (as the Mac's Restart), not rewind's.
* Playing no longer waits some 20 seconds when a copy's server drops connections or its name doesn't resolve: racing copies looks names up through DNS-SD and tries a server's addresses at once, without blocking, so its deadline holds.
* On the Apple TV, playing starts one stream, not two (the first abandoned, but loading still), its cover shows in the info panel and Now Playing, and its start's steps are timed in the log.
* Placeholder icons for the Mac and the Apple TV (with its Top Shelf), a film stack on cinema red; with a compiled asset catalog, tvOS's search keyboard no longer faults looking for one.
* The Apple TV's player is presented by UIKit, ending SwiftUI's warnings as its controls animate, and the app redraws nothing while it goes to the background.
* Syncs that bring or send nothing new no longer say so ("Nothing new from …"); only exchanges with changes show.
* An Apple TV removed from its library no longer shows "NoLibrary" for good once paired again: leaving stops the refresh under way, problems clear when a device gains or leaves a library or a read succeeds, and errors are said in words.
* A new Apple TV shows by its own name when pairing (as its network name spells it, "Not Your AppleTV"), not as "Apple TV", which is all tvOS tells apps.
* Pairing codes show a digit to a box, in two groups of three, on the device showing one and in the Mac's field for typing one, so you can see how long a code is; the Mac pairs at the sixth digit and shakes off a wrong code.
* Quality, not rendition, in the code and docs too; the C interface's JSON says `quality` and `qualities` (a source's stored `rendition` field keeps its name).
* A device's home is its data directory: `--home` is now `--data`, and `CINEMATHEQUE_HOME` is `CINEMATHEQUE_DATA` (the old names are gone; on macOS, run `agent add` again so the launch agents use the new option).
* The command-line app says quality, as the apps do: `--rendition` is now `--quality`, and listings print `quality`.
* The Mac app's covers no longer wobble as the inspector opens or closes: they keep one size, and the grid reflows whole columns.
* The Mac app asks before deleting a download that's the only copy, from its trash button, an episode's menu, or Delete Season's Downloads.
* The Mac app calls a rendition its quality ("Quality: 4K HDR"), and removes, rather than forgets, an unavailable folder's videos; the command-line app's `source forget SOURCE DIR` is now `source remove SOURCE --unavailable DIR`.
* A lexicon in AGENTS.md: one word for each thing, everywhere.
* The command-line peer is the command-line app (in `cli/`); its `peer` commands are now `device add`, `device forget`, and `device list` (which also lists devices nearby and addresses added). A device, not a peer, everywhere; addresses added carry over.
* The command-line app's `user` commands are now `person` (`person show`, `person pair`); a person, not a user, everywhere.
* Pairing a device as another of yours is pairing, not linking, everywhere; a device pairs with one running this build or later.
* The command-line app's `repo` commands are now `source` (`source add`, `source list`, …), `playhead` is `progress`, folders `crawl` finds empty are unavailable rather than absent, and `crawl` counts copies, not holdings: as the apps say.
* One name for each thing, on the Mac and in the command-line app alike: Download Next Episodes (was Keep Next Episodes, beside Keep, which keeps a download); Start from Beginning and Mark as Unwatched (both were Clear Progress); Delete for downloads; Refresh Library; devices, sources, and unavailable in place of peers, repositories, and not found.
* On the Mac, C turns captions on or off while playing.
* The Mac opens on the shelf you had open last, or at first on In Progress.
* Downloading a series' next episodes now counts them from the one you're watching, so the next one downloads while this one plays (from a download; a stream holds downloads back until it stops, so as not to slow it).
* On the Mac, the library's shelf title no longer shows through the title bar over playback.
* In Settings, each source's row opens it (a folder on this Mac in the Finder, a server's listing in your browser) and removes it, once you confirm, by buttons of its own or its context menu; Refresh Library is a button with a circular arrow.
* A folder on the Mac that isn't there, as an SMB share unmounted, no longer fails crawling ("bad status"): it's served empty, so its titles show dimmed until it's back. Crawling's messages name each source as Settings does and say what went wrong plainly ("the server has nothing at that address").
* On the Mac, playing in the library's window, the title bar's row shows the window buttons, Back, and what's playing (the movie, or the series with the episode's code and name), centered, fading in and out with the controls as QuickTime Player's does.
* The Mac downloads, to watch without a network: a movie, a season, or a series' next 1, 3, 5, or 10 episodes, downloaded as you watch; into `~/Movies/Cinematheque` (or a folder chosen in Settings, under Downloads), served to your other devices, and played from the file first. A watched episode is deleted only once another source still has it, at once or as space is needed, and 20 GB is always left free. A Downloaded shelf lists what's here, and covers carry a mark.
* On the Mac, Played splits into In Progress and Watched. Watched also lists what you've watched since downloading it, marked: an hourglass on what goes once its space is needed (another source has it), orange on the only copy, for you to delete or keep. Watched movies, and seasons downloaded whole, go the same way as a series' episodes; Keep exempts one.
* The command-line peer downloads: `title download` fetches a title's, a season's, or an item's files from whichever copy answers first, resuming one cut short.
* A server answering `HEAD` for a file no longer trips its check that the whole body was sent (a crash in debug builds).
* On the Mac, a season's From Start leaves the Play row for the episode list's heading, as a small Restart button, shown only when Play would start elsewhere.
* A device removed from the library finds out when it next syncs, forgets the library and its identity, and returns to setup, saying so, to pair again as a new device; the command-line peer gains `device list`, `device remove`, and `library leave`.
* On the Mac, the inspector drops Start Over; a reset button beside a progress meter forgets where you stopped instead, so Resume becomes Play.
* A Mac sharing a folder on an unplugged drive still shares its library and its other folders, and the folder again once it's back; Settings › Sources shows it as not found.
* A video whose first copy doesn't load (on a Mac that's asleep, or a drive that's unplugged) plays from another; before playing, the copies' servers are asked at once, so one on a sleeping Mac costs a second or so rather than a long wait.
* An unplugged drive's folder (its mount point, empty) is noted as unavailable with one change, its videos kept and shown dimmed, rather than each removed and added back; they clear once it's back, after a year, or when you forget it in Settings › Sources (or `repo forget` on the command line). The command-line peer's `INDEX` lists directories, and is rebuilt once a minute old, so drives plugged in or unplugged show.
* Syncing runs both ways: a device sends its changes to each peer it syncs with, so one behind a NAT keeps a server it can reach in step, and a change reaches your other devices in one exchange; the nudges that asked peers to come and pull are gone.
* Devices join a library and link to your other devices only by pairing: the command-line peer drops `user secret`, `user link`, `library join FOUNDER`, and `member add`, so your secret is never shown, and its offers print the port they listen on, for pairing from off your network.
* Removing a member and removing a device work the same way, and a device that took a removed device's later changes before hearing of the removal now repairs them, as it did for removed members.
* The Mac's Settings are People, Devices, and Sources: you rename yourself in People, and this Mac in Devices, which also holds sharing, pairing new devices, and addresses.
* The Mac app no longer hangs at launch, or on renaming or changing what it serves, when its server misses being stopped.
* In Settings, Sources' Videos column is right-aligned, the Rendition and Address explanations sit beside their fields, and People and Devices remove with a symbol.
* Settings › People & Devices can remove one of your devices: what it changes from then on is refused, and no peer syncs with it.
* The apps sync a minute after they last did, whenever they come to the front, and when another device says it changed something: pausing or leaving playback, marking something watched, or changing the watchlist syncs at once and tells your other devices, which pick it up within seconds. The Mac's status bar (and briefly, the Apple TV's corner) says how many changes were last received from and sent to which device.
* Progress gauges count running time: a season's (and on the Apple TV, a series') shows how much of it you've played, episodes partway included.
* Covers on the watchlist show a star.
* The watchlist takes a series season by season: listing one season no longer lists them all.
* Playback fades to black while the video warms up, paused, then plays and fades in as its sound rises; leaving it fades to black as its sound falls; every fade takes half a second, and resuming replays the 4 seconds before where you left off, on the Mac and the Apple TV (which waits for the display to switch modes while paused).
* A device's playheads, watchlist, and crawls reach your other devices again if an older build signed its certificate wrongly: it signs a fresh one.
* A device just paired moves from New Devices into Your Devices at once, and your devices can be renamed in Settings (an Apple TV can only call itself "Apple TV").
* Settings › Sources has Add Folder… and Add Server… buttons in place of the Add menu.
* Inviting by code: a new Mac pairs in setup by a code it shows, as the Apple TV does, and someone joining (Join Someone's Library, or `library join`) shows a code a member enters (or `member pair CODE`), which adds them. Settings › People & Devices lists new devices in a section of their own, and the apps no longer show or take keys.
* Pairing by code: a new Apple TV (or peer, by `user pair`) shows a 6-digit code, and entering it on the Mac (Settings › People & Devices › Add a Device, or `user pair CODE`) sends it your secret and the library, instead of typing the 64-character secret.
* A season's Play resumes the episode in progress, if any, rather than starting the first unwatched one.
* On the Mac, Space plays or pauses, F toggles full screen, M mutes, and the arrows skip 10 seconds back and forward; Back shows only while the pointer moves.
* Season covers show whole, not cropped, on the Mac and the Apple TV.
* Start Over, a rewind button before Resume on the Mac and Apple TV, plays from the beginning; on the Mac, a season's From Start joins them, leftmost.
* The watched button shows a title's state, Watched or Unwatched; pressing it toggles.
* On the Mac, Play fills the library's window, and Back or Escape returns to the library; ⌘-click or ⌘-double-click plays in a window of its own, as before.
* A Played shelf on the Mac and Apple TV: everything you've started or watched, including a season with an episode partway; Remove from Played, in a title's context menu, forgets its playheads.
* The Apple TV sets up as tvOS apps do, with the Mac's choices: use it on another of your devices, join someone's library, or start a new one.
* A device whose library copy was lost (as an Apple TV's cache may be purged) gets its own playheads back from your other devices.
* The Mac and Apple TV players are one multiplatform app.
* Redesigned Settings in the Mac app: names instead of keys for people and devices (shared with the library), folders on this Mac as sources (including mounted shares, such as /Volumes/Shared), people nearby waiting to join, and keys and addresses (for peers beyond a NAT) under Advanced.
* The Mac and Apple TV apps serve the library while they run, so other devices pull their changes, such as playheads, directly; the core can serve several folders, each at a path of its own.
* An Apple TV player: it links and joins by typing, syncs and crawls for itself, browses by cover art (Home, Movies, Series, Watchlist, Search), and plays full screen, choosing the best rendition it can play, keeping playheads as the Mac does.
* Peers advertise themselves by their device's key, not their user's, so a user's devices no longer take one another for themselves and skip syncing.
* Refreshing syncs with peers first, then crawls, so a new device gets the catalog already described and its crawl finds nothing to redo; every player, iOS and tvOS ones too, may crawl.
* Playheads: stopping within a video's first 10 seconds forgets its position, so rewinding to the start sticks; the Mac app saves where it's paused or closed, not only every 10 seconds, and no longer reopens player windows at launch.
* Blurred placeholders: each cover's ThumbHash, made by the core while describing (from a JPEG's DC coefficients, without decoding it in full) and shared with the library, so tiles show a likeness of their covers at once.
* The Mac app has a status bar over the bottom of the grid, fading when idle, with the shelf's counts, a refresh's progress, and problems (never alerts); it refreshes the library at launch, and keeps the top title in place when the inspector opens or closes.
* The Mac app gives each shelf its own scroll position, so Series no longer shows a blank grid after scrolling far into Movies.
* Faster covers: describing runs after crawling, in parallel across every repository, committing as it goes, and the Mac app shows titles, then covers, as they're found, describing those on screen first; a full first crawl of about 5,400 movies and seasons took 36 seconds on a LAN. Each title is described once, not once per repository holding it.
* Specials without episode numbers (beside a series' episodes, or in a Specials folder) list as a series' specials, not as movies; titles keep dots between digits ("11.22.63"); camera files named by number go to review.
* The Mac app lists a movie's versions (e.g., Unrated), by their names as written, and plays a season's queue through to its end, marking each episode watched.
* Browsing by cover art in the Mac app, from sidecar images or MP4s' own covers, read by range request, with rudimentary metadata (descriptions, genres, release dates, durations, episode titles), an inspector to play from, season queues, and a watchlist.
* Setting up and managing a library from the Mac app: creating, joining, and linking; repositories, members, crawling, and syncing.
* First version: the Zig core (a replicated, signed library in SQLite; crawling repositories; the filename parser; sync over HTTP; DNS-SD discovery; a C interface), the command-line peer for macOS and Linux, and a macOS player.
