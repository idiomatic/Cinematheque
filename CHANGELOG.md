# Changelog

All notable changes to Cinematheque are recorded here.

## Unreleased

* Show Sync Activity is gone from the apps; each sync's exchanges that changed something are logged instead (`sync`, in Console). A device's push is noticed by the library's data version, and `cq_served` and `cq_received` are gone.
* Placeholders: added and removed only by the command-line app's new `want` commands (`want list`, `want add`, `want remove`); the apps and the web client show them but no longer add, change, or remove them (the Mac's Add Missing Title… and Edit…, the Apple TV's editing page, the web client's ＋ and ✎, and Remove from List). `cq_list_want` and `cq_list_edit_want` are gone.
* Command-line app: `serve --downloads` and the web client's Keep (its Keep buttons, a season's Keep choice, and the Kept shelf) are gone; only the Mac app keeps downloads for other devices. Download, to the browser's computer, stays.
* Sync: a device that took a removed device's later writes before hearing of the removal gets back what they overwrote by pulling everything once, in place of repair's own exchange; requests no longer carry a repair digest.
* Older builds' ways are dropped, so every device updates together: sync answers are always salted (requests no longer carry a flags byte); sources' old quality settings (From File Names, From the Folder Under It; the command-line app's `--quality-by`) and the one-time move of a device's own sources to port-free addresses (`cq_repo_move`) are gone; and the apps no longer rewrite an older build's bad certificate on opening, nor the Apple TV its Keychain's seeds.
* Every member is equal: the library's creator can be removed like anyone, and leaving never asks who takes over. Handing a library over (`library handover`, `library leave --to`) is gone, and `cq_remove_self` takes no successor. Update every device together: an older one still refuses others' removal of the creator's devices.
* Downloads: a series' watched episodes go as soon as they're watched, if another source holds them alike, not only once their space is needed; their servers are no longer asked first. The command-line app's `title download` is gone.
* Covers: before one loads, its tile shows a color of its own, not a blurred likeness; the core reads only a JPEG's width and height (`shape`), so rows are still laid out before covers load. `cq_thumbhash` and the web client's `/api/thumb` are gone.
* Apps' debug builds: the launch options no script used (`CINEMATHEQUE_CODE_FILE`, `PLAY`, `ITEM`, `LEAVE`, `PAGE`, `PAIR`, `PAIR_NAME`, and `SERVER`) are gone.
* Command-line app: `title review`, `title facets`, and aliases are gone; no player showed them.
* Mac: the status bar fades out before the grid's scroll bar, which it covered the bottom of.
* Mac: the room the Series shelf keeps at its end for the tallest band says, quietly, "This space is intentionally left blank."
* Covers: a season whose first episode's MP4 has no cover takes the next one that has (another quality's, or the next episode's), as Black Sails' and Homeland's did; and of files alike, a server's is preferred to a Mac's own drive's, whose covers go missing as it's unplugged (Andor's second season).
* Covers: a drive crawled alone (as it's plugged in, or added) no longer replaces covers described from another source's MP4s with its MKVs' none; and a cover described from an MKV gives way to an MP4 crawled beside it. Refresh Library brings back covers lost so.
* A drive plugged in or unplugged is noticed at once: the Mac app hears it from macOS, front or not (it looked every 5 seconds, and only while in front), and `serve --media` sees the mount table change within 2 seconds, on Linux too, its `INDEX` built afresh and its media crawled.
* Mac: a folder of its own that goes (a drive unplugged) or comes back is looked through at once, so its videos show as unavailable, and Play passes over them, right away, on every device, and downloads waiting on it start when it's back. The Download menu marks a quality whose copies are all unavailable with a clock ("when Archive1841 is back"), still to be chosen, and a quality chosen and still coming is canceled in its place in the menu, the others offered beside it; and a season's trash button cancels its episodes still coming, even before any is here.
* A download whose copies are away (an unplugged drive, a server off) waits quietly for them to come back, not "Downloading failed: not found."
* Downloads, and the web client's relay of a file to a browser, run at the drive's or network's speed: a deadline on every read, canceled as each came, held a download from a Mac's own drive to 8 MB/s; now one watchdog gives each mebibyte its minute.
* Titles keep a word that's a scene token too ("Madame Web", "The Darjeeling Limited" were cut short), and a season folder naming its series (`Ahsoka Season 1`) holds that series' episodes, not a movie per episode.
* A source's quality setting is one by default, From Names and Folders: each file's quality is the nearest of its name (if it says a resolution) and its folders that says one, a folder by its whole name (`Video/TV Shows/1080p/30 Rock/…` is 1080p; `Movies/HQ 1080p30 Surround/…` stays HQ 1080p30 Surround). Sources added before keep their setting until you choose it.
* A device's own folders and downloads (the Mac's, and `serve --media` and `--downloads`) have port-free addresses (`cqdevice://` and the device's key), reached where the device says it serves: a new port is one write, not every copy removed and added again, as when another program took the Mac's port. Those the apps added by port move there once, keeping their copies, unavailable ones too.
* Mac: a download plays (and shows as downloaded) from this Mac's own record of its file, so one whose source has gone, as an unplugged drive's whose source moved, is still found, with no writes to the shared library.
* Apps: an MKV or ISO, which Apple's players can't play, opens in VLC if it's installed (Play says "Play in VLC"), on the Mac, iPhone, and Apple TV.
* Screenshots made again, with the mock library's art for Sanctuary Moon's seasons 7 to 12.
* Command-line app: `person set --name` and `device set --name` name this person and their devices; `library leave` removes this device and syncs before forgetting, as the apps' Leave This Library does (`--to` handing the library over if need be).
* Settings, on every app and the web client, say when each of your devices was last seen: when it last synced with this one.
* Mac: "Not enough space to download…" shows as soon as downloading finds it, rather than only once it ends, which syncing could put off for good.
* Documentation: the README is short (what it is, getting it, getting started), and [docs/guide.md](docs/guide.md) the whole user guide, each fact once, the stale ones brought up to date.
* Downloads' progress counts only what's being fetched: a file left for want of room no longer fills the bar, as if it had come. The Mac's Downloads pane says only what's downloaded and the space free, leaving the space available for downloads to the Download menus.
* Screenshots made again: series tiles showing their season to watch, and the mock library's longer Sanctuary Moon. The screenshot script answers macOS's Local Network prompt before sizing the Mac's window, as the prompt keeps it from resizing.
* Mac: Settings' Devices and Sources line their names up, each row's symbol in one width.
* The mock library's Sanctuary Moon has 28 seasons of 419 episodes, and Worldhoppers two of 12, as the Murderbot Diaries have them; its first twelve seasons and its special have art of their own.
* Apple TV, iPhone, and Mac: an episode's or version's watched mark lines up down the list, whatever the width of each runtime.
* Mac: a tile's Lists › New List… names the new list in place in the sidebar, with the title on it, rather than in an alert.
* Mac and web client: a series' "{n} seasons" no longer counts its specials as a season.
* A series' tile shows the cover of its season to watch (the first not watched through), not always its first season's, in every app and the web client; its progress bar still counts the whole series.
* iPhone: no more alerts for names and codes. A new device's code is typed in a field under it; New List names the list in place at the top of the Lists tab; a list is renamed by its page's title; New List… from a title's Lists menu is a small sheet.

* Apps: debug builds build the core ReleaseSafe too, so Zig's standard library failing a connection attempt no longer crashes them.
* Apps: a change made while a sync or crawl holds the library (a name, a watched mark, progress) waits its turn and is made once it's free, instead of failing with "Couldn't change the library while it's busy". Syncing holds it for one device at a time, not all of them.
* Mac: Settings' Devices shows each device by its kind's symbol (an iPhone, an Apple TV, a laptop…), not all as a desktop computer.
* Apple TV and web client: adding a device or someone is a section of Your Devices or People, listing the devices offering nearby, as the iPhone and the Mac have it, not a page or panel of its own.
* `CINEMATHEQUE_DNSSD` sets the service type devices announce and look for; the mock library's tools use `_cinemock._tcp`, so its devices and a person's own never see each other, or each other's offers to pair.
* iPhone: adding a device or someone is a section of Your Devices or People, as the Mac's Devices pane has New Devices, not a page of its own. The launch option `CINEMATHEQUE_PAIR` now takes the device's name too (`CINEMATHEQUE_PAIR_NAME`), never entering its code for another.
* Syncing skips a removed device still announced on the network (as one gone back to setup may be), and the apps no longer report a device that can't be reached as they catch up ("nothing answered there"), as a phone whose app is closed can't be.
* Web client: Settings' Sources leaves devices' downloads out, as the apps' do (its own are under Kept).
* Command-line app: `serve` syncs as it starts, and leaves a library that removed it, as the apps do, its web client set up afresh; it went on serving the library's catalog and sources.
* Devices by Address is gone from the apps' and the web client's Settings (and `cq_device_address_add` and `cq_device_address_forget` from the C interface): devices sync whenever they share a network. The command-line app's `device add URL` still reaches a device elsewhere, and addresses already added are still synced with.
* Web client: Settings in the apps' order and groups (Names, Sharing, Library), its names This Server and You, as the apps' This iPhone and This Apple TV, and devices by address under Your Devices.
* Mac: Settings hides nothing behind Advanced toggles: Sources' Look for videos, a source's quality and names to skip, and Devices' devices by address and Show sync activity always show.
* A folder on a Mac is shown by its own name on every device ("firefly › Archive1841", not "archive1841"): its address keeps its name whole, and its source records it (`cq_repo_name`), so folders added before, at slugs, are named too.
* A file whose name says no quality takes its folder's, or that folder's folder's, up to its source, the nearest naming one: a drive laid out as a NAS's share (`Video/TV Shows/1080p/30 Rock/…`) reads as 1080p from its root, not Other.
* Devices record their models (`cq_set_device_model`; `cq_devices` gives each one's model and kind), and the iPhone's Your Devices shows each by its kind's symbol, with "This iPhone" or its model under its name, as Apple's own list of devices does.
* An iPhone or Apple TV no longer names itself "iPhone" or "Apple TV" at its first launch, as iOS and tvOS tell apps, over the host's name pairing gave it ("lint"). On the iPhone, Settings' Devices by Address moves under Your Devices; Skip Names Starting With is a heading over its field; and address fields start empty, with an example, taking an address without `http://`.
* iPhone: an icon, the Mac's and the Apple TV's red gradient and film stack, drawn by draw-icons.swift.
* iPhone: Settings, as iOS's own apps' are: names; people and your devices, each added by the code a device shows (those offering nearby listed with their models); sources, servers added by address, each with its quality, names to skip, and unavailable folders; Refresh Library, Look for New Videos, and Show Sync Activity; devices by address; and Leave This Library….
* Web client: Add Someone's and Add a Device's panel closes by an × in its corner, or Escape, in place of Cancel and Done.
* A device offering to be added says what kind it is ("iPhone 13 mini", "MacBook Pro (14-inch, 2021)"), shown under its name on the Mac, the Apple TV, and the web client. `cq_pair_offer` takes its model.
* Web client: Settings' rows act one way, by symbols at their end (a pencil renaming, a ⊖ removing, an × forgetting; a trash only where files are deleted), as sources' already did, in place of words on some rows and symbols on others.
* Mac: the sidebar is at most 320 points wide and the grid at least 480, so dragging the sidebar's divider no longer takes the grid down to nothing; and View › Show Sidebar, ⌃⌘S.
* Mac: the inspector keeps its width between launches, and opens at its own ideal width at last (its width, set within it, went unheeded, so it opened at SwiftUI's default).
* Command-line app: a device it can't reach as it syncs after a change (a phone whose app is napping) is said once, not at every sync, until it answers again.
* Devices listen on IPv6 as well as IPv4, so `http://localhost:8008/` answers at once in a browser that tries `::1` first (it was refused there, the browser falling back, or not, to `127.0.0.1`).
* Web client: a favicon, the app's icon filling its square, at 16, 32, and 48 pixels.

The web client's Settings show them so, with their counts of files, the web's external-link symbol opening each in a new window, and a ⊖ removing it, devices' downloads left behind too.

Only a browser on the server's own computer (or through an SSH tunnel) acts as it, unless `--permissive`; others are told so.

* Downloads: whether another source holds a download alike is asked within a minute; a crawl stops at 4 million entries as it finds them, not once a level's done.
* Apple TV: Add a Device and Add Someone are one page given what to do, not told by a flag.

* Apple apps: leaving the library, or being told it was removed, waits out what's under way (stopping a download, kept to resume), rather than failing as busy, and nothing more of a refresh runs after.
* Security: a sync's answer is sealed under a key salted afresh, so a request replayed by an eavesdropper is never answered under the same key and nonces, which leaked private progress; a sync's sealed request must arrive within 30 seconds and the rest a mebibyte a minute, and the replica opens only once it checks out, so no one holds connections without being a member's; a stranger's batches held back each cost at least 4 KiB and are checked once, so a fake device can't keep a sync checking signatures for minutes.

* Downloads: deleting a title or season while an earlier file downloads stops its own files too; they came anyway, and stayed.
* Security: a cutoff (`revoked`) past any sequence number a stream could reach is refused; a member's device could write one that crashed every device that synced with it.

* On the Mac, a season's From Start leaves the Play row for the episode list's heading, as a small Restart button, shown only when Play would start elsewhere.
* A device removed from the library finds out when it next syncs, forgets the library and its identity, and returns to setup, saying so, to pair again as a new device; the command-line peer gains `device list`, `device remove`, and `library leave`.
* On the Mac, the inspector drops Start Over; a reset button beside a progress meter forgets where you stopped instead, so Resume becomes Play.
* A Mac sharing a folder on an unplugged drive still shares its library and its other folders, and the folder again once it's back; Settings › Sources shows it as not found.
* A video whose first copy doesn't load (on a Mac that's asleep, or a drive that's unplugged) plays from another; before playing, the copies' servers are asked at once, so one on a sleeping Mac costs a second or so rather than a long wait.
* An unplugged drive's folder (its mount point, empty) is noted as unavailable with one change, its videos kept and shown dimmed, rather than each removed and added back; they clear once it's back, after a year, or when you forget it in Settings › Sources (or `repo forget` on the command line). The command-line peer's `INDEX` lists directories, and is rebuilt once a minute old, so drives plugged in or unplugged show.
* Syncing runs both ways: a device sends its changes to each peer it syncs with, so one behind a NAT keeps a server it can reach in step, and a change reaches your other devices in one exchange; the nudges that asked peers to come and pull are gone.
* Devices join a library and link to your other devices only by pairing: the command-line peer drops `user secret`, `user link`, `library join FOUNDER`, and `member add`, so your secret is never shown, and its offers print the port they listen on, for pairing from off your network.
* Removing a member and removing a device work the same way, and a device that took a removed device's later changes before hearing of the removal now repairs them, as it did for removed members.
* The Mac's Settings are People, Devices, and Sources: you rename yourself in People, and this Mac in Devices, which also holds sharing, pairing new devices, and addresses.
* The Mac app no longer hangs at launch, or on renaming or changing what it serves, when its server misses being stopped.
* In Settings, Sources' Videos column is right-aligned, the Rendition and Address explanations sit beside their fields, and People and Devices remove with a symbol.
* Settings › People & Devices can remove one of your devices: what it changes from then on is refused, and no peer syncs with it.
* The apps sync a minute after they last did, whenever they come to the front, and when another device says it changed something: pausing or leaving playback, marking something watched, or changing the watchlist syncs at once and tells your other devices, which pick it up within seconds. The Mac's status bar (and briefly, the Apple TV's corner) says how many changes were last received from and sent to which device.
* Progress gauges count running time: a season's (and on the Apple TV, a series') shows how much of it you've played, episodes partway included.
* Covers on the watchlist show a star.
* The watchlist takes a series season by season: listing one season no longer lists them all.
* Playback fades to black while the video warms up, paused, then plays and fades in as its sound rises; leaving it fades to black as its sound falls; every fade takes half a second, and resuming replays the 4 seconds before where you left off, on the Mac and the Apple TV (which waits for the display to switch modes while paused).
* A device's playheads, watchlist, and crawls reach your other devices again if an older build signed its certificate wrongly: it signs a fresh one.
* A device just paired moves from New Devices into Your Devices at once, and your devices can be renamed in Settings (an Apple TV can only call itself "Apple TV").
* Settings › Sources has Add Folder… and Add Server… buttons in place of the Add menu.
* Inviting by code: a new Mac pairs in setup by a code it shows, as the Apple TV does, and someone joining (Join Someone's Library, or `library join`) shows a code a member enters (or `member pair CODE`), which adds them. Settings › People & Devices lists new devices in a section of their own, and the apps no longer show or take keys.
* Pairing by code: a new Apple TV (or peer, by `user pair`) shows a 6-digit code, and entering it on the Mac (Settings › People & Devices › Add a Device, or `user pair CODE`) sends it your secret and the library, instead of typing the 64-character secret.
* A season's Play resumes the episode in progress, if any, rather than starting the first unwatched one.
* On the Mac, Space plays or pauses, F toggles full screen, M mutes, and the arrows skip 10 seconds back and forward; Back shows only while the pointer moves.
* Season covers show whole, not cropped, on the Mac and the Apple TV.
* Start Over, a rewind button before Resume on the Mac and Apple TV, plays from the beginning; on the Mac, a season's From Start joins them, leftmost.
* The watched button shows a title's state, Watched or Unwatched; pressing it toggles.
* On the Mac, Play fills the library's window, and Back or Escape returns to the library; ⌘-click or ⌘-double-click plays in a window of its own, as before.
* A Played shelf on the Mac and Apple TV: everything you've started or watched, including a season with an episode partway; Remove from Played, in a title's context menu, forgets its playheads.
* The Apple TV sets up as tvOS apps do, with the Mac's choices: use it on another of your devices, join someone's library, or start a new one.
* A device whose library copy was lost (as an Apple TV's cache may be purged) gets its own playheads back from your other devices.
* The Mac and Apple TV players are one multiplatform app.
* Redesigned Settings in the Mac app: names instead of keys for people and devices (shared with the library), folders on this Mac as sources (including mounted shares, such as /Volumes/Shared), people nearby waiting to join, and keys and addresses (for peers beyond a NAT) under Advanced.
* The Mac and Apple TV apps serve the library while they run, so other devices pull their changes, such as playheads, directly; the core can serve several folders, each at a path of its own.
* An Apple TV player: it links and joins by typing, syncs and crawls for itself, browses by cover art (Home, Movies, Series, Watchlist, Search), and plays full screen, choosing the best rendition it can play, keeping playheads as the Mac does.
* Peers advertise themselves by their device's key, not their user's, so a user's devices no longer take one another for themselves and skip syncing.
* Refreshing syncs with peers first, then crawls, so a new device gets the catalog already described and its crawl finds nothing to redo; every player, iOS and tvOS ones too, may crawl.
* Playheads: stopping within a video's first 10 seconds forgets its position, so rewinding to the start sticks; the Mac app saves where it's paused or closed, not only every 10 seconds, and no longer reopens player windows at launch.
* Blurred placeholders: each cover's ThumbHash, made by the core while describing (from a JPEG's DC coefficients, without decoding it in full) and shared with the library, so tiles show a likeness of their covers at once.
* The Mac app has a status bar over the bottom of the grid, fading when idle, with the shelf's counts, a refresh's progress, and problems (never alerts); it refreshes the library at launch, and keeps the top title in place when the inspector opens or closes.
* The Mac app gives each shelf its own scroll position, so Series no longer shows a blank grid after scrolling far into Movies.
* Faster covers: describing runs after crawling, in parallel across every repository, committing as it goes, and the Mac app shows titles, then covers, as they're found, describing those on screen first; a full first crawl of about 5,400 movies and seasons took 36 seconds on a LAN. Each title is described once, not once per repository holding it.
* Specials without episode numbers (beside a series' episodes, or in a Specials folder) list as a series' specials, not as movies; titles keep dots between digits ("11.22.63"); camera files named by number go to review.
* The Mac app lists a movie's versions (e.g., Unrated), by their names as written, and plays a season's queue through to its end, marking each episode watched.
* Browsing by cover art in the Mac app, from sidecar images or MP4s' own covers, read by range request, with rudimentary metadata (descriptions, genres, release dates, durations, episode titles), an inspector to play from, season queues, and a watchlist.
* Setting up and managing a library from the Mac app: creating, joining, and linking; repositories, members, crawling, and syncing.
* First version: the Zig core (a replicated, signed library in SQLite; crawling repositories; the filename parser; sync over HTTP; DNS-SD discovery; a C interface), the command-line peer for macOS and Linux, and a macOS player.
